Exercises & practice

Reading about 3D printing gets you only part of the way. Hands-on practice does the rest. This part of the compendium has 69 exercises in five tracks, ninety quiz questions, fourteen test models and a print journal template, so that every hour at the printer teaches you something specific.

The level map

How an exercise is built

Every exercise has the same structure, so you always know where to look. Read the goal and the materials first, then work through the steps. Try to meet the success criteria before you open the hint, and look at the worked solution last, to compare it with your own result and not to copy it.

A good way to work

Plan a short session: read an exercise, start a print, and use the waiting time to read the linked chapter. Most exercises need one print or two. Avoid doing three at once, because mixing results from different experiments is a common way to learn the wrong thing.

📓 Print journal: document your prints

Experienced makers keep a journal. It is the difference between “that print was good somehow” and “PETG at 245 °C with 0.4 mm retraction at 40 mm/s gave zero strings”. Many exercises ask you to record numbers, and a journal is where they live. It can be a notebook, a spreadsheet or a text file; only the habit matters.

What to record

FieldExampleWhy it matters
Date and project2026-03-02 · cable clip v3Lets you find the print again and see trends

ResultFit OK, 3 strings, 18 g, 41 minNumbers allow comparison between prints

Pro tip
    
Write the print time and the filament mass from the slicer into the journal before the print starts, then add the real values afterwards. After ten prints you will estimate costs and schedules much more accurately. The calculators can help you to convert grams into cost.

All levels at a glance

  

    
Level 1 · Beginner
First print checklist, calipers, first-layer quality, printer parts, loading filament, slicer preview, layer height and infill experiments, a cable clip, brim, temperature, supports, spool labels, safety audit and a first model in Tinkercad.

    
Level 2 · Intermediate
Temperature tower, retraction, flow, bridging, overhangs, tolerance coupons, parametric box, drying, material comparison, supports, seams, fillets, orientation, heat-set inserts and two-color printing.

    
Level 3 · Advanced
Pressure advance, input shaping, volumetric flow, print-in-place hinges, snap-fits, threads, custom profiles, minimal-waste multi-material, annealing, scripted CAD, gears, mechanisms, time versus strength, finishing and G-code.

    
Level 4 · Expert
Compliant mechanisms, topology-driven brackets, assemblies, batch optimization, Cp/Cpk, design of experiments, reverse engineering, fixtures, material characterization, large parts, casting and open-source release.

  • Numbers beat feelings: use calipers, a scale and a journal.
