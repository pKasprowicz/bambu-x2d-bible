How to use this page

1) Run the first five checks, which fix more than half of all failures. 2) Find your symptom with the symptom picker or the decision tree. 3) Work through the causes in the order listed and change one thing at a time. 4) Log what worked. Settings names follow Bambu Studio/OrcaSlicer; PrusaSlicer and Cura use near-identical terms. For X2D-specific parts, see X2D maintenance & troubleshooting.

The first five checks for any failure

Most printing problems come from a short list of root causes. Before you tune a single setting, run through this list. It takes five minutes and saves hours of chasing symptoms.

  • 1 · The first layer. Is it squished evenly, with lines touching and no gaps or ridges? Is the plate clean (wipe with IPA, or warm water and dish soap, no finger oils)? See first layer & adhesion.
  • 2 · The filament. Is it dry and the right type in the right profile? Popping, hissing, fuzzy surfaces and stringing mean wet filament. See drying & storage.
  • 3 · The filament path. Does the spool unwind freely, with no tangles, kinks or sharp bends? Is the extruder gripping with no grinding and no clicking?
  • 4 · The nozzle. Is it clean (no burnt crust), the right size, tightened, and flowing a smooth, straight strand when you extrude by hand? Compare the flow to a fresh nozzle.
  • 5 · The profile. Are temperature, cooling and speed set for this material and layer height? Is volumetric flow within the hotend’s limit? See speed, cooling & quality.
The golden rule

Change one variable, print a small test (a 20 mm cube or a calibration tower takes 10–20 minutes), compare, and keep notes. Changing five things at once turns troubleshooting into guessing.

🟠 Extrusion & flow

1 · Stringing hairs between parts

Look for: thin hairs or cobwebs across open spaces after the nozzle travels.

Causes (most likely first)
  1. Wet filament (steam pushes plastic out during travel)
  2. Nozzle temperature too high
  3. Retraction too short or off, or travel too slow
  4. Slow travel speed / wiping disabled
  5. Material is naturally stringy (PETG, TPU)
Fix
  1. Dry the filament (PLA 45–55 °C for 6–8 h; PETG 60–65 °C for 6–8 h).
  2. Lower nozzle temperature in 5 °C steps until stringing fades but layer bonding stays good.
  3. Increase retraction in small steps (direct drive: 0.4–1.0 mm; Bowden: 2–5 mm). Enable wipe on retract and avoid crossing open areas.
  4. Raise travel speed (150–300 mm/s); run a retraction test tower.

Prevention: store filament sealed or in a dry box; use a calibrated profile per filament.

2 · Oozing / drooling plastic leaks from a resting nozzle

Look for: blobs on the surface, a thin line of plastic at the start of moves, a drip on the bed.

Causes
  1. Wet filament or too-hot nozzle (pressure remains in the melt chamber)
  2. Retraction disabled or too small
  3. Waiting on a hot nozzle (heat soak, filament changes)
  4. Worn or loose nozzle (leak around the thread)
Fix
  1. Dry filament; lower temperature 5–10 °C.
  2. Increase retraction, enable wipe and “retract on layer change”.
  3. Keep waiting times short, or retract before long pauses.
  4. Hot-tighten the nozzle (see maintenance) or replace it.

Prevention: use a purge/wipe move before printing and avoid keeping the nozzle hot while idle.

3 · Blobs and zits small bumps on the wall

Look for: pimples at the same spot every layer (seam) or random dots.

Causes
  1. Pressure not released at layer start/end (retraction, pressure advance too low)
  2. Over-extrusion or temperature too high
  3. Seam position set to “nearest” or “random” on a visible face
  4. Wet filament or dirty nozzle
Fix
  1. Calibrate pressure advance/flow dynamics, then flow ratio.
  2. Set seam to “aligned” or “back” and hide it at a sharp edge; use scarf seam if available.
  3. Enable “wipe before outer wall” and reduce temperature 5 °C.
  4. Dry filament; clean the nozzle.

Prevention: run the calibration sequence whenever you change filament.

4 · Under-extrusion not enough plastic

Look for: thin, gappy or missing lines, weak parts, rough infill, dotted lines in the first layer.

Causes
  1. Partial clog or worn nozzle
  2. Nozzle too cold or speed too high for volumetric flow limit
  3. Wet or bad-quality filament; wrong diameter
  4. Extruder tension/gear dirt or filament path friction
  5. Flow ratio too low
Fix
  1. Do a cold pull (procedure below) or replace the nozzle.
  2. Raise temperature 5–10 °C or lower max volumetric speed to the filament’s limit.
  3. Dry filament; measure its diameter in three places.
  4. Clean the extruder gear and check feed tension; remove tight bends.
  5. Re-run flow calibration.

Prevention: replace nozzles on schedule and keep filament dry.

5 · Over-extrusion too much plastic

Look for: parts larger than designed, rough or bumpy walls, filled corners, ridged top surfaces.

Causes
  1. Flow ratio too high for this filament
  2. Wrong filament diameter in profile
  3. Temperature too high (low viscosity)
  4. Pressure advance too low
Fix
  1. Calibrate flow ratio (typical 0.94–1.0).
  2. Check diameter (1.75 mm) in the filament profile.
  3. Lower temperature 5 °C and tune pressure advance.

Prevention: keep per-filament profiles with calibrated flow.

6 · Gaps in the top surface top layers don’t close

Look for: parallel lines on top with slits you can see through to the infill.

Causes
  1. Too few top layers over sparse infill
  2. Under-extrusion on the top layers
  3. Infill too sparse or line spacing too large
  4. Top layers cooling too slowly (soft, sagging)
Fix
  1. Use 5–7 top layers (≥ 1 mm); increase top solid infill density.
  2. Raise flow slightly on top surfaces; fix under-extrusion.
  3. Use 15–25 % infill with a pattern that supports the roof (gyroid, cubic).
  4. Increase part-cooling fan on top layers.

Prevention: choose top-layer count by the infill spacing, not by habit.

7 · Pillowing top skin bulges between infill lines

Look for: pillow-like dips on the top face, strongest on large flat areas with low infill.

Causes
  1. Not enough top layers
  2. Too little cooling of the first top layers
  3. Too-hot nozzle or too-fast speed on top
  4. Sparse, widely spaced infill
Fix
  1. Add top layers (to ≥ 1 mm) and increase infill to 20–30 %.
  2. Boost fan on top layers; reduce temperature 5 °C.
  3. Enable ironing as a cosmetic top-up.

Prevention: match infill density to the roof span.

8 · Rough, holey top pitted or scratchy top

Look for: tiny holes, bumps or dragging lines across the final layer.

Causes
  1. Wet filament (steam pits)
  2. Over- or under-extrusion on the top layer
  3. Nozzle dragging on curled corners
  4. Dirty nozzle with burnt residue
Fix
  1. Dry filament and clean nozzle.
  2. Calibrate flow; use “ensure vertical shell thickness”.
  3. Enable “avoid crossing walls”, reduce overhang curling (more cooling).
  4. Slow top-surface speed (≈ 60–100 mm/s).

Prevention: clean the nozzle at the start of each session.

9 · Missing walls / gaps between perimeters thin features lack material

Look for: gaps in thin walls and fine details; walls that should join but don’t.

Causes
  1. Wall thickness not a multiple of the line width
  2. Feature thinner than the minimum the slicer can fill
  3. Under-extrusion
Fix
  1. Make walls 2 × line width (e.g. 0.84 mm for 0.42 mm lines) or enable “detect thin walls” / “arachne” wall generator.
  2. Increase the thin-wall width in CAD to ≥ 0.8 mm.
  3. Check flow and nozzle.

Prevention: design with nozzle multiples; see design principles.

10 · Burnt filament / dark flecks black specks in the plastic

Look for: black or brown specks, a darkened surface, a burnt smell, clogs that return quickly.

Causes
  1. Nozzle too hot or filament sitting hot too long (idling, slow prints)
  2. Residue from a previous high-temperature material
  3. Contaminated or dusty filament
  4. Wrong material profile (PLA at ABS temperatures)
Fix
  1. Lower temperature; avoid parking hot.
  2. Purge 100+ mm with a fresh filament; do a cold pull; replace the nozzle if specks persist.
  3. Clean the spool path (a dust-wiping sponge).

Prevention: always flush between materials with different temperatures.

11 · Moisture bubbling wet filament

Look for: crackling or hissing, tiny bubbles in the strand, stringing, rough and matte surfaces, weak layers.

Causes
  1. Filament absorbed humidity (nylon, PETG, TPU, PVA, PLA all do)
  2. Open spool storage for weeks
  3. Humid room and no dry box
Fix
  1. Dry at the material’s temperature (PLA ~50 °C, PETG ~65 °C, nylon ~80 °C, TPU ~55 °C, per maker).
  2. Print from a sealed box or the AMS 2 Pro with fresh desiccant.
  3. Check desiccant indicator color.

Prevention: see drying & storage.

12 · Clicking / grinding extruder the gear can’t push

Look for: ticking sounds, the gear spinning without feeding, filament shavings on the gear.

Causes
  1. Clogged or too-cold nozzle (back pressure)
  2. Printing faster than the hotend can melt
  3. Retraction too large or too frequent
  4. Spool tangle or too much friction
  5. Dirty gear / wrong tension
Fix
  1. Cold pull the nozzle; raise temperature by 5–10 °C.
  2. Cut max volumetric speed.
  3. Clean the gear with a brush; check the tension; reseat the filament.
  4. Lower retraction length.

Prevention: keep the filament path clean and don’t exceed the filament’s flow limit.

13 · Nozzle clog (full or partial) stream is thin, curling or absent

Look for: nothing extrudes, the strand curls sideways, or flow is weak and uneven.

Causes
  1. Debris or burnt residue in the nozzle
  2. Switching between very different temperatures without a purge
  3. Dust or abrasive wear (CF/GF filaments in a brass nozzle)
  4. Heat creep (softened filament in the cold zone)
Fix
  1. Do a cold pull (see below); repeat until the plug comes out clean.
  2. Clear the nozzle with a 0.35 mm needle at operating temperature (careful, hot).
  3. Replace the nozzle if it still flows poorly.

Prevention: purge between materials, use hardened nozzles for abrasives, schedule replacement.

14 · Heat creep jams that return after minutes

Look for: a good start, then clicking or a jam after 10–60 minutes, especially in hot enclosures.

Causes
  1. Heatsink fan failing, blocked or dusty
  2. Hot chamber or ambient (PLA in an enclosure at 40+ °C)
  3. Frequent long retractions pulling hot filament up
  4. Heatbreak/thermal paste issue
Fix
  1. Check that the hotend fan spins at full speed; clean the duct.
  2. Open the enclosure door or lower chamber temperature for PLA/PETG.
  3. Shorten retraction; print slower.
  4. Reassemble the heatbreak properly.

Prevention: keep hotend cooling clean; see maintenance.

🔵 Adhesion & warping

15 · First layer not sticking lines lift or drag

Look for: lines that do not bond, loops of plastic dragged by the nozzle, a print that detaches in the first minutes.

Causes
  1. Dirty plate (oil, dust, glue residue)
  2. Z offset too high (nozzle too far)
  3. Bed or nozzle too cold; first layer too fast
  4. Bed not level/warped (auto-leveling data stale)
  5. Wrong plate for the material
Fix
  1. Wash the plate with warm water and dish soap, then IPA; do not touch the surface.
  2. Re-run calibration; lower Z offset in 0.02 mm steps until lines are slightly flattened and touching.
  3. First layer: 50–60 °C bed (PLA), 70–80 °C (PETG), slow (20–30 mm/s), 0.2–0.3 mm height.
  4. Use glue stick for PETG/nylon on smooth plates (also prevents damage).

Prevention: a clean plate and a calibrated first layer. See first layer & adhesion.

16 · Warping corners curl up

Look for: corners or edges lifting, a gap under the part, cracked layers in tall parts.

Causes
  1. Material shrinks while cooling (ABS, ASA, nylon, PC, PET-CF)
  2. Drafts or a cold chamber
  3. Large flat footprint, sharp corners
  4. Bed too cool; poor adhesion
Fix
  1. Close the enclosure and let the chamber warm; stop part-fan cooling for ABS/ASA.
  2. Add a brim (5–10 mm); round the corners; use mouse ears on large parts.
  3. Raise bed temperature; apply adhesive; use an appropriate plate.

Prevention: use a heated chamber for high-shrink materials (X2D chamber up to 65 °C).

17 · Elephant foot flared first layers

Look for: first layers wider than the rest, mating parts that no longer fit.

Causes
  1. Nozzle too close / Z offset too low (squish)
  2. Hot bed (especially 70 °C+) softens lower layers under weight
  3. First layer too thick or too much flow
Fix
  1. Raise Z offset a little; use slicer’s “elephant foot compensation” (0.1–0.25 mm).
  2. Lower bed temperature by 5–10 °C after the first layers.
  3. Chamfer the bottom edge (0.4–0.6 mm × 45°) in CAD.

Prevention: chamfer the base of critical parts.

18 · Adhesion too strong part won’t release

Look for: chips of plate coating pulled off, difficulty removing PETG from smooth PEI, bent plate.

Causes
  1. PETG, TPU or nylon on smooth/high-adhesion plates
  2. Z offset too low or bed too hot
  3. No release agent (glue stick layer)
Fix
  1. Let the plate cool completely; flex a spring-steel plate.
  2. Use a thin layer of glue stick (acts as a release agent for PETG on smooth PEI).
  3. Raise Z offset slightly; lower bed temp.
  4. Use a textured plate for PETG.

Prevention: match plate and material; see first layer & adhesion.

19 · Spaghetti / detachment print becomes a plastic bird’s nest

Look for: the print is gone from the plate while the nozzle continues printing in the air.

Causes
  1. Adhesion failure of the first layers (see card 15–16)
  2. Part tipped by warping or by a collision with a curled edge
  3. Support failure under an overhang
  4. Clog or runout mid-print leading to broken layers
Fix
  1. Stop the print immediately (see the when-to-stop guide) and remove the mess before it wraps the nozzle.
  2. Clear nozzle and heater block of plastic; check for damage.
  3. Fix the root cause above before reprint; enable AI spaghetti detection if your printer has it.

Prevention: watch the first layer and use a brim for tall, thin or warp-prone parts.

🟣 Layers & structure

20 · Layer separation / delamination layers split apart

Look for: visible gaps between layers or parts that split along layer lines.

Causes
  1. Nozzle temperature too low for the speed
  2. Too much cooling (ABS/ASA/PC/nylon)
  3. Wet filament; under-extrusion
  4. Layer height too large for the nozzle (> 75 %)
  5. Cold, drafty chamber
Fix
  1. Raise temperature 5–10 °C; slow down.
  2. Reduce fan to 0–30 % for high-temp materials; close the enclosure.
  3. Dry filament; fix flow.
  4. Use layer height ≤ 60 % of the nozzle diameter.

Prevention: print functional parts hot and warm; consider annealing.

21 · Cracking vertical splits as the part grows

Look for: cracks that run along or across layers on tall or boxy parts, often at corners.

Causes
  1. Thermal stress: part cools unevenly (drafts, fan, cold chamber)
  2. Material with high shrinkage (ABS, ASA, PC, PA)
  3. Sharp internal corners (stress concentrators)
  4. Weak layer bonding (see delamination)
Fix
  1. Increase chamber and bed temperature; stop drafts; close the lid.
  2. Lower or disable the part fan.
  3. Add fillets (radius ≥ 1–2 mm) at internal corners and use more walls.

Prevention: enclosure and warm chamber for engineering materials.

22 · Layer shifting part jumps sideways

Look for: a clean offset in X or Y at a certain height; everything above is shifted.

Causes
  1. Collision with a curled edge or a knocked part
  2. Loose belt or pulley set screw
  3. Speed or acceleration too high; motor current too low (skipped steps)
  4. Toolhead cable or tube snagging
Fix
  1. Check for warped edges; fix warping or enable lift (Z hop) on travel.
  2. Tighten pulleys, check belt tension (see maintenance).
  3. Reduce acceleration and speed; check the cable path.

Prevention: inspect mechanics monthly; avoid crashes by keeping prints flat.

23 · Z-banding / wobble regular horizontal bands

Look for: repeating ripples at a fixed distance, or a wobbly column.

Causes
  1. Bent lead screw, loose or dirty Z coupler (on non-Bambu printers)
  2. Temperature or flow fluctuation
  3. Binding Z rods, missing lubrication
  4. Unstable frame
Fix
  1. Clean and lubricate Z mechanics; check straightness of rods/screws.
  2. Use steady temperatures; avoid fan surges.
  3. Tighten the frame and bed supports.

Prevention: keep Z mechanics clean and lubricated.

🟢 Surface & dimensions

24 · Dimensional inaccuracy parts are not the size they should be

Look for: consistent size errors (+/– 0.1–0.4 mm), or errors that depend on direction.

Causes
  1. Flow ratio too high (outer walls bloated)
  2. Material shrinkage (ABS 0.5–1 %, PA 1–2 %)
  3. Elephant foot, over-extruded corners
  4. Belt tension or steps/mm miscalibrated
Fix
  1. Calibrate flow and pressure advance first.
  2. Print a calibration cube, measure, and use XY size compensation or filament shrink factor.
  3. Verify motion calibration (see calibration).

Prevention: keep a tolerance coupon for each material.

25 · Holes too small screws and pegs won’t fit

Look for: holes below the designed diameter; screws bind.

Causes
  1. Perimeters slightly overfill small circles
  2. Over-extrusion or low-resolution polygon approximation
  3. Material shrinkage inside the hole
Fix
  1. Add 0.2–0.4 mm to hole diameters in CAD, or use hole compensation (e.g. “XY hole compensation”).
  2. Use “detect small circles” / slow small perimeters.
  3. Ream with a drill bit by hand.

Prevention: keep a clearance parameter in CAD. See tolerances & fits.

26 · Corner bulging corners balloon outward

Look for: swollen, rounded corners, especially on tall parts or fast prints.

Causes
  1. Pressure advance too low or not calibrated
  2. Corners are too hot because layers are short (high temp, low cooling)
  3. Acceleration or jerk too low/high, causing pressure surges
Fix
  1. Calibrate pressure advance (flow dynamics).
  2. Add cooling and slow small layers (“slow down for layer cooling”).
  3. Use “scarf” or aligned seam away from sharp corners.

Prevention: re-run PA after changing filament, temperature or nozzle.

27 · Seam bulges a vertical line where layers start and end

Look for: a visible zipper-like line on cylinders and smooth walls.

Causes
  1. Seam position nearest/random on a visible face
  2. Pressure not released at start/end of the perimeter
  3. Slight over-extrusion at the start point
Fix
  1. Align the seam to the back or paint a seam in an inside corner.
  2. Use scarf-joint seams and “wipe on loops” if available.
  3. Tune pressure advance and flow.

Prevention: design a shallow vertical groove where the seam can hide.

28 · Scarring / combing marks scratch lines across the top

Look for: thin scratches and tiny ridges on the top surface following travel paths.

Causes
  1. Nozzle travels across the top layer while hot (combing)
  2. Nozzle drags through slightly high top lines
  3. Over-extrusion on the top surface
Fix
  1. Enable “avoid crossing walls” and “z hop when retracting” (0.2–0.4 mm).
  2. Reduce flow on top layers; lower top-layer temperature.
  3. Iron the top surface.

Prevention: use top-layer settings tuned for quality.

29 · Unintended fuzzy or rough walls walls look like sandpaper

Look for: a grainy, random texture on walls even though fuzzy skin is off.

Causes
  1. Wet filament (steam bursts)
  2. Fuzzy skin accidentally enabled by a modifier or a profile
  3. Wobbling spool or unstable feed
  4. Too-hot nozzle plus fast outer walls
Fix
  1. Dry the filament.
  2. Check “fuzzy skin” in the process settings and modifiers.
  3. Smooth the feed path; slow outer walls to 60–100 mm/s.

Prevention: check your profile before blaming hardware.

🌸 Motion & vibration

30 · Ringing / ghosting echo ripples after corners

Look for: faint echoes of a sharp feature (text, a hole) on the wall after a corner.

Causes
  1. High acceleration or jerk for the frame’s stiffness
  2. Input shaping not calibrated (resonance compensation)
  3. Loose belts, loose frame, printer on a wobbly table
  4. Heavy toolhead or long moving mass
Fix
  1. Run vibration compensation / input shaping calibration; lower outer-wall acceleration.
  2. Tighten belts and screws; put the printer on a heavy, stable base with isolating feet.
  3. Lower outer wall speeds (100–150 mm/s) for finish.

Prevention: recalibrate after moving or transporting the printer. See speed, cooling & quality.

31 · VFA fine vertical stripes

Look for: fine regular vertical lines that change with print speed, not with layer height.

Causes
  1. Stepper microstep resonance at a particular speed
  2. Belt, pulley or bearing roughness
  3. Outer-wall speed in a resonance band
Fix
  1. Change the outer wall speed by ±20 mm/s and compare.
  2. Check belts and bearings; recalibrate motion.
  3. Print at consistent speed in the quiet band.

Prevention: tune outer wall speed on a test print.

🟠 Bridges, overhangs & supports

32 · Poor bridging strands droop across gaps

Look for: sagging, stringy undersides over gaps, rough bridge surfaces.

Causes
  1. Too little cooling on the bridge
  2. Nozzle too hot; bridge speed or flow wrong
  3. Span too long (> 30–50 mm)
  4. Wet filament
Fix
  1. Maximum fan for bridge layers; lower the temperature 5–10 °C.
  2. Use the slicer’s bridge flow ratio (start at 0.85–0.95) and slow bridge speed to 20–40 mm/s.
  3. Add a support or a mid-span pillar for long spans.

Prevention: design spans under 30 mm. See supports, bridges & overhangs.

33 · Sagging overhangs rough, drooping undersides

Look for: messy, shiny, drooping surfaces on steep overhangs.

Causes
  1. Angle beyond ~45–55° from vertical without supports
  2. Too hot / too little cooling
  3. Layer too tall or too fast
Fix
  1. Increase fan, lower temperature; use “slow down for overhangs”.
  2. Reorient the part or add supports.
  3. Add chamfers instead of horizontal ledges.

Prevention: design self-supporting angles (≤ 45°).

34 · Curling overhangs edges roll upward

Look for: overhang edges lifting into the nozzle path; clicking or scuffing sounds.

Causes
  1. Thin perimeters cooling unevenly and shrinking upward
  2. Too much flow on the overhanging line
  3. Insufficient cooling
Fix
  1. Increase cooling; lower the temperature; reduce speed on overhangs.
  2. Print with “reverse on odd” or “ensure vertical shell thickness”.
  3. Use supports for the edge.

Prevention: a hotter bed hurts overhangs, so keep it modest for PLA.

35 · Poor support interface rough underside above supports

Look for: rough or pitted underside after support removal.

Causes
  1. Z gap too small or too large
  2. No interface layers; bad interface density
  3. Support too dense near the part
Fix
  1. Use 2–3 interface layers and a Z gap of one layer (0.1–0.2 mm).
  2. Slow interface speed; add cooling.
  3. Use dedicated interface material (e.g. PVA, BVOH, or support filament) when possible.

Prevention: match support material to part material.

36 · Supports welded to the part won’t release

Look for: supports that tear chunks off the part when removed.

Causes
  1. Z gap of 0 or less than a layer
  2. Same material at high temperature, especially PETG
  3. Support density too high near the contact
Fix
  1. Increase Z gap to 0.2–0.3 mm; use a looser support pattern at the interface.
  2. Use a different support material or tree supports.
  3. Cut with flush cutters and a hobby knife; use heat or a thin blade.

Prevention: test a support-gap tower. See post-processing.

🌈 Multi-material & feeding

37 · Blobs at tool change lumps where colors switch

Look for: small blobs or streaks of the old color near the transition.

Causes
  1. Oozing while idle or during the swap
  2. Insufficient purge volume or poor pressure advance after the swap
  3. Wipe or prime tower too small or too far
Fix
  1. Enable the prime/wipe tower and increase flush volume between very different colors.
  2. Reduce standby temperature differences; check retraction.
  3. Use “wipe into infill / object” for waste reduction only after the basics work.

Prevention: see multi-color & multi-material.

38 · Color bleed old color tints the new one

Look for: muddy colors, tinted whites, streaks after a swap.

Causes
  1. Purge volume too low (especially dark → light)
  2. Residual filament in the nozzle
  3. Old color in the AMS path or hotend
Fix
  1. Raise flush volumes for dark → light; print light-to-dark where possible.
  2. Purge manually before starting.
  3. Print a clean test and re-check.

Prevention: order colors from light to dark; plan purge volume.

39 · AMS / feeder jams filament will not load or unload

Look for: feed errors, filament chewed or stopped, repeated retry loops.

Causes
  1. Tangled or crossed spool, snag at the spool edge
  2. Brittle or wet filament snapping
  3. Kinked or dirty PTFE tube, debris in the feeder
  4. Bent filament tip or blob on the end
Fix
  1. Untangle and respool; cut a clean, tapered tip.
  2. Remove the tube; pull the filament through by hand.
  3. Clean the feeder gear and path; replace worn tubing.

Prevention: store filament dry; avoid brittle old spools. For the X2D see X2D maintenance & troubleshooting.

40 · Spool tangles loops cross and bind

Look for: spool stops turning; sudden under-extrusion mid-print.

Causes
  1. Loose end not secured after use
  2. Crossed windings on a re-spooled roll
  3. Spool resting too high or at a sharp angle to the tube
Fix
  1. Pause; unwind slack and clear the cross.
  2. Use a smooth path with minimal friction.
  3. Secure the end in the spool clip every time.

Prevention: always clip the end; store spools sealed.

Nozzle clog: the cold-pull procedure

A cold pull (also called an atomic pull) removes the carbonized residue and foreign particles that a normal purge leaves behind. Do it when flow is partial, when filament will not extrude after a swap, or when you see dark flecks.

  1. Prepare. Use a cheap, clean, medium-stiff filament (nylon or PLA). Preheat to 240–260 °C and push filament by hand until it flows freely.
  2. Cool. Set the nozzle to the pull temperature (about 90–100 °C for PLA, 120–140 °C for PETG, 140–160 °C for nylon). Keep light pressure on the filament.
  3. Pull. When the nozzle reaches the target, pull the filament out in one firm motion. The tip should show a molded nozzle-shaped end with dark specks.
  4. Repeat. Cut off the tip and repeat 3–5 times until the tip is clean.
  5. Verify. Extrude 50 mm at normal temperature; the stream should be even and straight.
Careful: the nozzle is hot for steps 1–2. Do not pull on a printer with a hotend that needs tools to open (follow your manufacturer’s manual for the X2D; use the guided unload/nozzle procedures). If the pull fails, replace the nozzle (see maintenance).

When to stop a print

Not every flaw is fatal. Stopping early saves filament and protects the printer, but stopping too early wastes good prints. Use this traffic-light guide.

Safety first: if you see smoke, smell burning plastic, notice scorched wiring or see sparks, power off and unplug the printer. Do not restart until the cause is found.

A repeatable diagnosis method

  1. Describe the symptom precisely. Where on the part (first layer, one side, everywhere, only tall sections)? When did it start? Is it regular or random?
  2. Separate mechanics, material and settings. Regular patterns point to mechanics; random blobs and bubbles point to material; changes between prints point to settings or hardware wear.
  3. Check the cheap things first. Dry filament, clean plate, clear nozzle, tight belts, correct profile for the filament actually loaded.
  4. Change one variable and print a small test. A 20 mm cube or a calibration tower takes minutes; a full failed print wastes hours.
  5. Write it down. A short log (date, filament, change, result) saves you from repeating the same experiment. The maintenance log has a template.
Rule of thumb: if a problem appears suddenly on a profile that worked yesterday, suspect something that changed physically: a new spool, a worn nozzle, a moved printer, a dirty plate, humid weather.

Key takeaways

  • Change one thing at a time and write down what you changed.
  • Most problems come from five causes: wet filament, dirty plate, wrong Z offset, wrong temperature, mechanical looseness.
  • Match the symptom to a card, test the most likely cause first, and verify with a small test print.
  • Stop failing prints early; protect the nozzle and the build plate.
  • Keep a maintenance habit so that problems do not appear: see maintenance.
