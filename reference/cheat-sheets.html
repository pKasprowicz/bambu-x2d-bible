How to use these sheets

Each sheet fits on roughly half a page. Print the ones you use most and pin them next to the printer. Where a number is a range, start in the middle and adjust. Always follow the filament maker’s label when it differs from the table.

Stay under the spool’s limit

Cardboard spools survive ~50–60 °C; plastic spools usually 65–80 °C. A dryer with a lower maximum (the AMS 2 Pro tops out at 65 °C) cannot dry nylon or PC fully: use a dedicated dryer or a hot-air filament dryer for those. Signs of wet filament: popping or hissing, steam, bubbles, stringing, rough surface. See drying & storage.

3 · Layer height & nozzle rules of thumb

NozzleLayer heightLine widthBest for
0.2 mm0.05–0.150.22–0.26Miniatures, fine text
0.4 mm0.08–0.300.42–0.50General purpose
0.6 mm0.15–0.450.62–0.75Fast, strong parts
0.8 mm0.20–0.600.82–1.0Large, draft, vases
  • Layer height ≈ 25–75 % of the nozzle diameter. Below 25 % the plastic is squashed and unreliable; above 75 % layer bonding suffers.
  • Line width ≈ 100–125 % of nozzle diameter; wider lines are stronger and faster.
  • Halve the layer height → double the time (roughly) and hide more layer lines.
  • Strength: thicker layers and wider lines usually bond better than tiny ones.
  • Details smaller than the nozzle cannot be printed; keep features ≥ 1× line width, walls ≥ 2 lines.

5 · Tolerance cheat sheet for design

Gaps are per side unless noted. Values assume a calibrated 0.4 mm nozzle printer; measure a test coupon on your machine and adjust.

FitGap (per side)Use
Press / interference−0.05 to +0.05 mmPins into holes, bearings in housings (check by test print)
Snug / transition0.10–0.15 mmSnap fits, lids that should stay put
Sliding0.15–0.30 mmDrawers, hinges, parts that move by hand
Loose0.30–0.50 mmPrint-in-place joints, easy assembly
Print-in-place clearance (vertical gaps)0.30–0.50 mm; down to 0.2–0.25 on tuned printersChains, hinges, captive balls
  • Minimum wall: 2 line widths (0.8–1.2 mm for a 0.4 nozzle).
  • Smallest hole that prints reliably: about 1.5–2 mm (drill out for accuracy).
  • Smallest pin: 2–3 mm diameter.
  • Text: ≥ 1 mm line thickness, 0.6 mm relief height.
  • Max unsupported bridge: 20–30 mm (longer with tuning).
  • Overhang limit: ≈ 45° without supports; 60° with good cooling.
  • Chamfer instead of fillet at the bottom edge (0.5 mm × 45°) to defeat elephant foot.
  • Holes shrink by 0.1–0.3 mm; add compensation in CAD.

  • Self-tapping into plastic: works for M3 and larger with 2+ mm of wall; fine for a few assembly cycles only. Use inserts for repeated use.

  • Taps in plastic: go slow, back out often, no oil on nylon-bound parts (use water or dry).

Magnet pockets (disc magnets)

  • Pocket diameter = magnet diameter + 0.1–0.2 mm (press fit) or + 0.3 mm (glued). Depth = magnet height + 0.1 mm.
  • Leave 0.4–0.6 mm (2–3 layers) of plastic between the magnet and the mating face; thinner is stronger but fragile.
  • Pause the print at the pocket’s top layer, insert the magnet, resume. Check polarity first with a marked reference magnet.
  • Secure with a drop of cyanoacrylate or epoxy for anything that is pulled hard.
  • Keep magnets away from pacemakers, credit cards and electronics; heat above ~80 °C weakens common neodymium magnets.

11 · First-layer one-pager

  • Plate washed (soap, then IPA); not touched afterwards
  • Correct plate for the material
  • Nozzle clean, no blob; filament purged
  • Auto-leveling / Z offset calibrated
  • First layer height 0.2–0.3 mm, speed 20–50 mm/s
  • Bed temperature right for the material (sheet 1)
  • Watch the first 3 minutes of every print
  • Too high: separate round lines, gaps, peeling.
  • Good: lines slightly flattened, touching, smooth.
  • Too low: ridged, translucent, rough, ripples, elephant foot.
  • Adjust Z by ±0.02 mm at a time.

g ↔ oz1 oz = 28.35 g

mm³ ↔ cm³1 cm³ = 1000 mm³
Filament Ø 1.75 mm cross-section≈ 2.405 mm²
Filament Ø 2.85 mm cross-section≈ 6.38 mm²
  • Volumetric flow (mm³/s) = line width × layer height × speed. Example: 0.42 × 0.20 × 150 = 12.6 mm³/s.
  • Max speed = max flow ÷ (line width × layer height). With 15 mm³/s: 15 ÷ (0.42 × 0.20) ≈ 178 mm/s.
  • Filament length (mm) = volume (mm³) ÷ 2.405 (1.75 mm filament).
  • Mass (g) = volume (cm³) × density (g/cm³).
  • Print time ≈ path length ÷ average speed, plus accelerations, travel and layer overhead (often +20–40 %).
  • Cost = grams × (price per kg ÷ 1000) + energy + wear.

Solvent and dust safety

Work outdoors or with strong ventilation, wear nitrile gloves and eye protection, and keep solvents away from flames. Sand wet or use a mask: plastic dust, especially carbon-fiber, should not be inhaled. See post-processing.

18 · Safety one-pager

  • Never leave a printer running where you cannot reach it or hear a smoke alarm; install a smoke detector in the room.
  • Ventilate when printing ABS, ASA, nylon, PC and resins; consider an enclosure with filtration.
  • Nozzle (up to 300 °C) and bed (up to 110 °C and above) cause instant burns; wait for cooling before touching.
  • Power off and unplug before opening electronics or the hotend.
  • Use flush cutters and scrapers away from your body; wear eye protection when removing supports.
  • Do not use printed parts for food contact, structural safety or electrical insulation without proper evaluation.
  • Keep a fire extinguisher (ABC or CO₂) accessible; never use water on an electrical fire.

19 · Pre-flight checklist

Model & slicer

  • Model is manifold, oriented for strength and supports
  • Right printer, nozzle size and plate selected
  • Filament profile matches the spool in the machine
  • Walls, infill, top/bottom set for the application (sheet 4)
  • Supports and brim set where needed
  • Layer preview checked (no gaps, no islands)

Printer

  • Plate clean, correctly seated
  • Nozzle clean; no old plastic on the bed or purge area
  • Filament dry, spool turning freely, no tangles
  • Enough filament for the whole print (weight estimate + 10 %)
  • Chamber/lid set for the material; printer ventilated
  • Camera or monitoring on; stay near for the first layer

If anything looks wrong during the first layer: stop, fix, restart. It is cheaper than any other time to fix it.

Key takeaways

Start from the table values, calibrate in order, change one variable at a time, keep filament dry, and use the troubleshooting sheet before blaming the printer. Print this page and keep the sheets you use next to the machine.
