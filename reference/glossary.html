#

3MF
3D Manufacturing Format: a zipped, XML-based project file that stores one or more meshes plus units, colors, materials and, in slicer-specific variants, print settings and plate layouts. It carries more information than STL, and Bambu Studio saves projects as 3MF.
3D printing
Everyday name for additive manufacturing: building a physical object layer by layer from a digital model. See the compendium overview.

A

ABS
Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene: a tough, heat-resistant thermoplastic that warps and fumes. Printed at about 240–260 °C with a heated chamber. See Engineering plastics.
Acceleration
How quickly the toolhead changes speed, in mm/s². High acceleration shortens print time but excites vibration and ringing. Limited by frame stiffness and input shaping.
Adaptive layer height
A slicer feature that varies layer height along the part: thin layers on curves and details, thick layers on vertical walls. It saves time while keeping detail. See Speed, cooling & quality.
Additive manufacturing
The industrial term for building objects by adding material, in contrast to subtractive manufacturing (cutting away). FDM, resin printing and powder-bed fusion are all additive processes.
Adhesion (bed adhesion)
How well the first layer sticks to the build plate. Too little causes warping and failed prints; too much makes parts hard to remove. See First layer & adhesion.
AMS
Automatic Material System: Bambu Lab’s multi-spool filament feeder that automates color and material changes, filament loading and, in newer models, drying. See AMS 2 Pro and Multi-color printing.
AMS 2 Pro
A Bambu Lab AMS generation with a heater, fan and desiccant for drying filament in place, up to 65 °C. Drying needs a 6-pin power connection and is weaker while printing. See AMS 2 Pro.
AMS HT
A single-spool AMS variant with a higher drying temperature than the AMS 2 Pro, intended for materials that need more heat. It requires external power for drying. Check Filament compatibility for current details.
Amorphous
A polymer whose chains are randomly tangled with no crystal order (PLA as sold, PETG, ABS, PC). Amorphous plastics soften gradually above their glass transition. See Materials overview.
Annealing
Heating a finished part below its melting point and cooling it slowly to relieve stress or raise crystallinity, increasing heat resistance. Often causes shrinkage. See Everyday plastics.
Anisotropy
Direction-dependent properties. FDM parts are strongest along the extrusion path and weakest between layers (Z). See Strength & orientation.
Arachne
A wall generator that varies extrusion width to fill thin features without gaps, as opposed to the older classic generator that uses fixed-width lines.
Arc fitting
Replacing many short straight G-code segments with fewer G2/G3 arc commands, reducing file size and stutter. See G-code & firmware.
ASA
Acrylonitrile styrene acrylate: like ABS but UV and weather resistant, so preferred outdoors. See Engineering plastics.
Auto bed leveling (ABL)
Probing the bed at several points so the printer compensates for tilt or warp by adjusting Z during printing, forming a bed mesh. Bambu printers perform it automatically before a print.
Auxiliary nozzle
On a dual-nozzle printer such as the Bambu X2D, the second nozzle. Its feed path and speed limits differ from the main nozzle, and some filaments (for example TPU) are restricted to the main one. See Dual-nozzle printing.

B

Bambu Studio
Bambu Lab’s slicer, a fork of PrusaSlicer/Slic3r with multi-material and cloud features. See Slicer settings.
Bed mesh
A map of the bed’s height at probe points, used to keep the first layer consistent when the bed is not perfectly flat. Produced during auto bed leveling.
Bed slinger
A printer whose bed moves back and forth along Y, as on many Cartesian designs. Heavy prints and high acceleration are limited by the moving mass. Contrast CoreXY.
Benchy
3DBenchy, a tugboat-shaped test model packed with overhangs, bridges, small holes and curves, widely used to judge print quality.
Blob (zit)
A small lump of excess plastic on a surface, typically at the seam where extrusion pauses. Reduced with pressure advance, retraction and seam placement. See Troubleshooting.
Bottom layers
Solid layers printed at the base of a part, above the first layer, giving a closed, flat underside.
Bowden
An extruder system with the motor on the frame and a PTFE tube carrying filament to the hotend. Lighter toolhead, but poor for flexible filaments. Contrast direct drive.
Brass nozzle
The standard nozzle: excellent thermal conductivity but soft, so it wears quickly with abrasive fillers such as carbon fiber or glow pigments. Use a hardened nozzle for those.
Bridge
A span printed between two supports with nothing underneath. Works best printed straight, slowly or with strong cooling. See Supports, bridges & overhangs.
Brim
A flat ring of extra lines around the first layer, connected to the part, that increases bed contact and reduces corner lifting. Removed after printing. Compare skirt.
Build plate (bed)
The surface on which the part is printed: smooth PEI, textured PEI, cool plate, engineering plate, and so on, usually heated. See First layer & adhesion.
Build volume
The maximum size of a printable part, stated as X × Y × Z, often reduced in practice by purge areas or skirts and by dual-nozzle limits.
BVOH
Butenediol vinyl alcohol copolymer: a water-soluble support material that prints more easily than PVA but is also extremely hygroscopic. See Support materials.

C

CAD
Computer-aided design: software used to model parts, either parametrically (Fusion, FreeCAD, OnShape) or as meshes. See the CAD modeling guide and parametric workflow.
Calibration
Measuring and adjusting printer behavior: flow, pressure advance, temperature, dimensions. See Calibration.
Carbon fiber (CF)
Short chopped fibers mixed into a polymer to increase stiffness and reduce shrinkage; abrasive and requires a hardened nozzle. See Composites.
Cartesian
A kinematic layout in which each axis moves independently (X, Y, Z). Simple and robust; contrast CoreXY.
Chamber
The enclosed print space of a printer. A warm chamber reduces warping; the Bambu X2D can actively heat it to 65 °C. See Engineering & chamber.
Chamfer
A bevelled edge, typically 45°. Chamfering the bottom edge of a part avoids elephant’s foot and improves printability of overhangs.
Clog
A blockage in the hotend or nozzle. Causes include debris, heat creep, burnt filament and wet filament. Often cleared by a cold pull. See Troubleshooting.
Cold pull
Cleaning method: heat a nozzle with filament, cool it to near the softening point and pull the filament out, bringing debris with it. Use unfilled nylon, PLA or PETG; do not use fiber-filled filament.
Color change
Switching filament mid-print, either manually at a layer or automatically through an AMS. Requires purging and creates flush volume waste.
Composite
A material with a matrix polymer plus fillers (fibers, minerals, metals), such as PA-CF. See Composites.
Cooling fan (part cooling)
A fan that blows air onto freshly extruded plastic to solidify it quickly, which is essential for PLA overhangs and bridges but harmful for ABS or nylon layer bonding. See Speed, cooling & quality.
CoreXY
A kinematic arrangement in which two motors with crossed belts drive the toolhead in X and Y while the bed moves only in Z. Allows fast, stable motion; used by Bambu printers.
Creep
Slow, permanent deformation of a plastic under a constant load, even below its yield strength, and faster at higher temperatures. PLA creeps noticeably.
Crystallinity
The fraction of a polymer in ordered, crystalline regions. Higher crystallinity gives more stiffness, chemical resistance and a defined melting point, with higher shrinkage. See Polymer science.
Curling
Edges of a layer lifting upward, usually at overhangs, because cooling shrinkage pulls them. Mitigated by cooling and slower speeds.

D

Delamination
Layers separating from each other, caused by low temperature, too much cooling, wet filament or a cold chamber. Visible as cracks along layer lines.
Density
Mass per unit volume. Typical filament densities: PLA ≈ 1.24 g/cm³, PETG ≈ 1.27, ABS ≈ 1.04, nylon ≈ 1.1, CF-filled materials slightly higher. Needed to convert volume to weight and cost. See Calculators.
Desiccant
A drying agent such as silica gel or molecular sieve that adsorbs water vapor, used in dry boxes and in the AMS 2 Pro. See Drying & storage.
DfAM
Design for additive manufacturing: shaping parts to exploit and respect the process, for example avoiding supports, choosing orientation and using lattices. See Design principles.
Dimensional accuracy
How closely a printed dimension matches the model, affected by shrinkage, flow, calibration and geometry. See Tolerances & fits.
Direct drive
An extruder mounted on the toolhead, a short distance from the hotend. Allows precise retraction and reliable printing of flexible filaments. Contrast Bowden.
DLP
Digital light processing: a resin printing technology that cures a whole layer at once with a projector. Not FDM.
Dovetail
A tapered, interlocking joint that resists pulling apart. Common in print-in-place and multi-material designs. See Joints & fasteners.
Dry box
A sealed container with desiccant (and sometimes a heater) that stores filament dry and may feed it directly to the printer. See Drying & storage.
Dual nozzle
A printer with two independent nozzles that print different materials or colors without swapping filament in a single hotend, reducing purge waste. See Dual-nozzle printing.
Duty cycle
The fraction of time a heater or fan is switched on. A heater at 100 % duty for long is working at its limit and may not hold temperature.

E

Elastic modulus
See Young’s modulus.
Elephant’s foot
A bulge at the bottom of a part caused by the first layers being squashed outward by heat and weight. Cured by first-layer tuning, slightly lower bed temperature or a chamfer.
Enclosure
A housing around the printer that holds heat, blocks drafts and contains fumes. Essential for ABS, ASA, nylon and PC.
Engineering plastic
A thermoplastic with higher mechanical, thermal or chemical performance than commodity plastics, such as nylon, PC and PEEK. See Engineering plastics.
ESD-safe
Electrostatic-discharge safe: a material with controlled resistivity (typically 10⁴–10⁹ Ω) that bleeds static charge. See Specialty filaments.
Exclude object
A firmware and slicer feature that lets you cancel one failed object on a plate and continue printing the rest.
Extruder
The mechanism, with motor and gears, that pushes filament into the hotend. Types: direct drive and Bowden.
Extrusion width
The width of the line of plastic laid down, usually 100–120 % of the nozzle diameter. Wider lines are stronger and faster; narrower lines give detail.

F

FDM / FFF
Fused deposition modeling (a trademark) or fused filament fabrication: melting a thermoplastic filament and depositing it layer by layer. The process this compendium covers.
Filament
The thermoplastic wire fed into an FDM printer, usually 1.75 mm diameter (tolerance ±0.02 mm) wound on a spool. See Materials overview.
Filament runout
The spool is empty or the filament is broken or tangled. A sensor pauses the print so you can load more; with an AMS it may switch to another spool of the same material.
Fillet
A rounded internal corner that reduces stress concentration and improves strength. See Strength & orientation.
Firmware
The software running on the printer’s controller that interprets G-code, controls motion, heaters and safety. See G-code & firmware.
First layer
The initial layer printed on the bed. Its quality decides adhesion and often the whole print. See First layer & adhesion.
Flexible filament
Elastomeric filament such as TPU, measured in Shore hardness. See Flexibles & specialty.
Flow Dynamics
Bambu Lab’s name for its pressure advance calibration. It sets a K value per filament so extrusion pressure lag at corners and starts is compensated. See Calibration.
Flow ratio
A multiplier (often ≈ 0.95–1.0) applied to the extrusion amount to correct for filament diameter and material behavior. Also called extrusion multiplier. See Calibration.
Flush volume
The amount of filament purged (in mm³) when changing color or material so the new color comes out pure. Depends on the color pair; dark-to-light needs more. See Multi-color printing.
Fuzzy skin
A slicer option that adds random jitter to the outer wall to create a textured, grippy, layer-hiding surface.

G

G-code
The text instructions (G0/G1 moves, M104 heating, and so on) sent to the printer; produced by the slicer. See G-code & firmware.
Gantry
The moving frame that carries the toolhead across the bed. In CoreXY machines it moves in X and Y.
Ghosting
A faint echo of a feature on the surface, caused by vibration after sharp direction changes. See ringing.
Glass transition (Tg)
The temperature at which an amorphous polymer changes from hard and glassy to soft and rubbery. Printed parts lose stiffness near it; PLA is about 60 °C. See Polymer science.
Gradient filament
Filament co-extruded or dyed so its color changes gradually along its length. See Specialty filaments.
Grid infill
A crossing-line infill pattern, simple and fast; its lines overlap at crossings, which can cause bumps.
Gyroid
A wavy, three-dimensional infill pattern with equal strength in all directions and no overlapping crossings. A popular default.

H

Hardened nozzle
A nozzle made of hardened steel (or tungsten carbide or with a ruby tip) that resists wear from abrasive filaments, with lower thermal conductivity than brass. See Materials overview.
HDT
Heat deflection temperature: the temperature at which a standard bar bends a set amount under a defined load. A practical indicator of heat resistance; differs from Tg.
Heat break
The thin-walled tube in a hotend that separates the hot zone from the cold zone to prevent heat creep.
Heat creep
Heat traveling upward and softening filament too early, causing clogs, especially during slow prints or retractions.
Heatbed
A heated build platform that improves adhesion and reduces warping.
HEPA
A high-efficiency particulate air filter, used in printer enclosures to capture ultrafine particles. It does not remove all gases; combine with carbon.
High-flow hotend
A hotend and nozzle designed to melt more plastic per second, measured as maximum volumetric flow. See Speed, cooling & quality.
HIPS
High-impact polystyrene: a plastic used as a support for ABS, dissolvable in limonene. See Support materials.
Homing
Moving axes to endstops (or a sensor) to establish the printer’s known position before printing.
Hotend
The assembly that melts filament: heat break, heater block, thermistor and nozzle.
Hygroscopic
Tending to absorb water from the air. Nylon, PVA, TPU, PC and PETG are hygroscopic. See Drying & storage.
Hydrolysis
A chemical reaction in which water breaks polymer chains at high temperature, permanently weakening materials like PC, PET and nylon when printed wet.

I

IDEX
Independent dual extruder: a printer with two toolheads that move independently, enabling duplicate or mirror printing.
Infill
The internal structure inside the walls, defined by pattern and density (0–100 %). Controls strength, weight and time. See Strength & orientation.
Input shaping
A motion technique that cancels resonance by adding counter-vibrations to commanded movement, allowing higher acceleration with less ringing.
Interface layers
See support interface.
Ironing
An extra pass of the nozzle over the top surface with very little extrusion to smooth it.
Island
A part of a layer that has no connection to the layers below, such as the start of a new feature in mid-air. Needs support.
Isotropic
Having the same properties in all directions; the opposite of anisotropic. Printed parts are not perfectly isotropic.

J

Jerk
The instantaneous change in velocity at a corner (older firmware) or the rate of change of acceleration (physics). Too high causes vibration. See junction deviation.
Jig
A custom tool that holds or guides a workpiece during assembly or machining; 3D printing makes jigs cheap. See Applications.
Junction deviation
A firmware method for limiting cornering speed based on corner angle, replacing the older jerk setting.

K

K factor
The value tuned in pressure advance or linear advance that scales how much extra pressure the extruder adds on acceleration.
Kinematics
How motor motions map to toolhead motion: Cartesian, CoreXY, delta, and so on.
Kerf
The width of material removed by a cutting process; in 3D printing, the equivalent is the gap you design to allow for parts to fit. See Tolerances & fits.

L

Layer adhesion
The strength of the bond between consecutive layers, determined by temperature, cooling and material. It sets the Z strength of a part.
Layer height
The thickness of each layer, typically 0.08–0.4 mm for a 0.4 mm nozzle. Thinner means finer detail and longer print time.
Layer line
The visible ridge between layers, a signature of FDM prints, reduced by thinner layers, ironing or finishing. See Post-processing.
Layer time
The time to print one layer. Short layer times let plastic stay hot; slicers slow down to guarantee a minimum for cooling.
Lead screw
A threaded rod that moves the Z axis. Lead screws can produce Z banding if misaligned.
LiDAR
A laser scanner used on some Bambu printers to inspect the first layer and calibrate flow. Availability depends on the model; check the X2D documentation.
Lightning infill
A minimal infill pattern that only supports top surfaces, saving time and material at the cost of strength.
Linear advance
A firmware feature equivalent to pressure advance, adjusting extrusion for pressure in the nozzle.
Linear rail
A precision guide that carries an axis smoothly with little play. Compared with rods and wheels, it has higher stiffness.
Lithophane
A thin, translucent 3D print whose varying thickness reveals an image when backlit.

M

M-code
A G-code command for machine functions rather than motion, such as M104 (set nozzle temperature) or M106 (fan speed).
MakerWorld
Bambu Lab’s online repository of models and print profiles, integrated with Bambu Studio and Bambu Handy.
Manifold (watertight)
A mesh with a clearly defined inside and outside, with every edge shared by exactly two faces. Slicers need manifold meshes. See Files & meshes.
Material profile
A slicer preset holding temperatures, flow, cooling and speed limits for one filament. See Settings & profiles.
Melting point (Tm)
The temperature at which crystalline regions of a semi-crystalline polymer melt (PA6 ≈ 220 °C). Amorphous polymers have no sharp melting point, only a Tg and a flow range.
Mesh
A 3D model made of triangles, as in STL. Compare parametric CAD.
Modifier
A slicer object that applies different settings to a region of a model, such as higher infill around a screw boss.
Mouse ears
Small, thin discs added at corners of the first layer to resist lifting. Like a mini-brim.
Multi-material printing
Using more than one filament in a print, for colors, supports or properties. See Multi-color printing.

N

NFC / RFID tag
A chip on Bambu Lab spools that the AMS reads to identify filament type, color and settings automatically.
Non-manifold
A mesh with holes, zero-thickness faces or edges shared by more than two faces, which confuses slicers. Fix in a mesh repair tool.
Nozzle
The tip of the hotend through which plastic is extruded. Diameter (0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 mm) sets detail and flow. See Settings & profiles.
Nylon (PA)
Polyamide: a tough, wear-resistant, hygroscopic polymer family (PA6, PA66, PA12). See Engineering plastics.

O

Oozing
Plastic dripping from the nozzle during travel moves, leaving blobs or hairs. Reduced by retraction, lower temperature and dry filament. See Troubleshooting.
OpenSCAD
A script-based, parametric CAD tool where models are described with code. See CAD modeling.
Orientation
How a part is positioned on the bed. It decides strength direction, support needs, surface quality and time. See Strength & orientation.
Outer wall
The outermost perimeter, which forms the visible surface. Often printed slowly for quality.
Overextrusion
Depositing more plastic than needed: swollen surfaces, bulging corners and rough tops. Fix with flow ratio calibration.
Overhang
A part of a model extending outward with little or nothing below it. Angles above about 45–60° from vertical usually need cooling or support. See Supports, bridges & overhangs.

P

PA
See nylon.
PAHT
High-temperature polyamide, usually fiber-reinforced, with heat resistance around or above 150 °C. See Engineering plastics.
Parametric design
A model defined by named dimensions and rules, so changing a value updates the whole part. See Parametric CAD workflow.
PC (polycarbonate)
A very tough, heat-resistant, hygroscopic amorphous polymer with a Tg near 145 °C that needs a hot nozzle and warm chamber. See Engineering plastics.
PCTG
A copolyester similar to PETG, usually tougher and clearer. See Everyday plastics.
PEI
Polyetherimide: both a high-performance filament (ULTEM) and the textured or smooth coating on most build plates, which sticks well when hot and releases when cool.
PEEK
Polyetheretherketone: an industrial high-performance polymer needing a nozzle above 380 °C, not suitable for desktop machines. See PEI and PEEK.
Perimeter (wall)
A closed loop of extrusion following the outline of a layer. The number of perimeters sets wall thickness and strength.
PET-CF
Carbon-fiber reinforced PET: stiff, dimensionally stable and relatively low-warp. See Engineering plastics.
PETG
Glycol-modified polyethylene terephthalate: tough, chemically resistant, easy to print and hygroscopic. See Everyday plastics.
Pillowing
Bumpy, sunken top surfaces caused by too few top layers or too little cooling over sparse infill.
PLA
Polylactic acid: the most popular filament; easy, stiff and low-warp but with a low Tg (≈ 60 °C) and creep. See Everyday plastics.
PLA-CF
PLA filled with carbon fiber for stiffness and a matte look; needs a hardened nozzle.
Polymer
A material made of long chain molecules built from repeating units. All filaments are polymers. See Polymer science.
PP
Polypropylene: a waxy, chemical-resistant, fatigue-resistant plastic with high shrinkage and poor adhesion. See Engineering plastics.
PPS
Polyphenylene sulfide: a chemical- and heat-resistant semi-crystalline polymer, typically carbon-filled (PPS-CF) for printing.
Pressure advance
A firmware correction that extrudes slightly ahead of acceleration and reduces extrusion ahead of deceleration, compensating for pressure lag in the nozzle. Called Flow Dynamics in Bambu software.
Prime tower
A sacrificial tower on the plate used to purge and stabilize flow after a filament change. See Multi-color printing. Also called a wipe tower.
Print-in-place
Printing movable assemblies (hinges, chains, gears) as one piece with clearance between parts. See Design patterns.
Profile
A saved set of slicer settings for a printer, filament or process. See Settings & profiles.
PTFE tube
A low-friction Teflon tube that guides filament from the spool or AMS to the printer.
Purge
Extruding filament to clear the old color or material, or to prime the nozzle. Waste is minimized by flush volume tuning.
PVA
Polyvinyl alcohol: a water-soluble support material that is very hygroscopic. See Support materials.

Q

Quad gantry leveling
A procedure on some printers where independent Z motors align the gantry parallel to the bed. Not used on every machine.
Quick-change nozzle
A nozzle or hotend designed to be swapped in seconds without tools, making material changes easier.

R

Raft
A thick, multi-layer base printed under a part to improve adhesion or insulate it from a warped bed. More material and a rougher bottom surface than a brim.
Resolution
The smallest feature a printer can render, limited by nozzle size, layer height and motor step size.
Resonance
A vibration frequency at which the frame amplifies motion, causing ringing. Measured and compensated by input shaping.
Retraction
Pulling filament back slightly before travel moves to reduce oozing and stringing. Distance (≈ 0.4–1 mm direct drive) and speed need tuning. See Calibration.
Ringing
Ripples on the surface after corners or sharp features, created by vibration. Reduced by lower acceleration or input shaping. See Speed, cooling & quality.
Rotation distance
A firmware value that says how far filament moves per extruder rotation. Errors here cause under- or overextrusion.
Ruby nozzle
A nozzle with a ruby insert at the tip that lasts a very long time with abrasive filaments.

S

Scarf seam
A seam that gradually ramps the wall’s height and flow along a diagonal to hide the joint.
Seam
The visible line where each layer’s outer wall starts and ends. Positioned at the back, in corners or randomly. See Speed, cooling & quality.
Semi-crystalline
A polymer with ordered crystalline regions in an amorphous matrix (nylon, PP, PET, PPS). Sharp melting, high shrinkage. See Polymer science.
Shell
The outer skin of a model made of perimeters, top layers and bottom layers.
Shore hardness
A durometer scale for rubbery plastics: Shore A for soft, Shore D for harder. TPU grades are labeled 85A–95A. See Flexible filaments.
Shrinkage
The reduction in size as plastic cools, from about 0.2 % (PLA) to over 1.5 % (nylon, PP). Causes warping and size errors. See Chamber temperature.
Silk PLA
PLA with additives that create a shiny, pearlescent finish. See Specialty filaments.
Skirt
One or more loops printed around the part, not touching it, to prime the nozzle and check flow. Different from a brim.
Slicer
Software that cuts a 3D model into layers and generates G-code: Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, PrusaSlicer, Cura. See Slicers.
Spaghetti
A failed print in which the plastic extrudes into the air as tangled strands, often after the part detaches. Detected by some printers’ cameras.
Sparse infill
Low-density infill (typically 5–30 %), providing support with less material.
Spiral vase mode
A single continuous outer wall with no seam and a solid base, forming smooth, thin-walled objects.
Stepper motor
A motor that moves in discrete steps, used for printer axes and extruders. Driven by microstepping drivers.
STEP file
A neutral CAD format that stores exact geometry (not triangles), used for exchange between CAD programs. See Files & meshes.
STL
Stereolithography file: the classic mesh format of triangles with no color or units. See Files & meshes.
Stringing
Fine threads of plastic between separate features, caused by oozing during travel. Cause: wet filament, too-hot nozzle, weak retraction. See Troubleshooting.
Subtractive manufacturing
Making parts by removing material (milling, turning). Contrast additive manufacturing.
Support
Temporary structures printed to hold up overhangs and bridges, removed afterwards. See Supports, bridges & overhangs.
Support interface
The top layers of a support, printed denser or from a different material to leave a smoother surface and release more easily. See Support materials.

T

Temperature tower
A calibration print with sections printed at different nozzle temperatures to find the best one for a filament. See Calibration.
Tg
Short for glass transition temperature.
Textured PEI
A powder-coated PEI build plate with a grainy finish that gives good adhesion and a matte bottom surface.
Thermal expansion
The tendency of materials to change size with temperature; relevant to bed, frame and part shrinkage.
Thermal runaway
A fault where a heater keeps heating uncontrollably because it can’t measure temperature correctly. Firmware protection shuts the printer down; never disable it.
Thermistor
A temperature sensor whose resistance changes with temperature, used for nozzle and bed.
Timelapse
A video built from photos taken after each layer. Needs a parking spot for the toolhead, which can add a purge or time cost.
Tolerance
The allowable variation in a dimension, and the clearance designed between mating parts (typically 0.15–0.3 mm on FDM). See Tolerances & fits.
Toolhead
The moving assembly that holds the extruder, hotend and fans.
Top layers
Solid layers closing the top of a part, giving a flat finish above the infill.
TPE
Thermoplastic elastomer: a family of rubbery thermoplastics that includes TPU.
TPU
Thermoplastic polyurethane: a flexible, abrasion-resistant filament, available in different Shore hardnesses. On the Bambu X2D it is printed with the main nozzle only. See TPU & flexibles.
Tramming
Adjusting the bed so it is parallel to the motion plane. Usually automated through ABL.
Travel move
A non-printing motion of the toolhead between two points. A source of stringing and oozing.
Tree supports
Branching supports that touch the model at few points, use less material and leave fewer marks than normal supports. See Supports, bridges & overhangs.

U

Underextrusion
Depositing less plastic than needed: gaps, thin walls and weak layers. Causes include clogs, wet filament, too-high speed, low temperature or a worn extruder gear.
UV resistance
Ability to withstand sunlight without cracking or yellowing. ASA and PETG fare well; PLA and ABS less so.

V

Vapor smoothing
Exposing a part to solvent vapor (acetone for ABS and ASA) so the surface melts slightly and layer lines disappear. See Engineering plastics.
VFA
Vertical fine artifacts: fine, regular vertical banding on walls, caused by motor steps and mechanical resonances.
Volumetric flow
Plastic volume extruded per second (mm³/s) = line width × layer height × speed. Limited by the hotend’s melting capacity. See Calculators.

W

Wall generator
The slicer algorithm that creates perimeters: Arachne (variable width) or classic (fixed width).
Warping
Corners or edges lifting from the bed as the part shrinks while cooling. Fix with a warm chamber, brim and better adhesion. See Chamber temperature.
Wet filament
Filament that has absorbed moisture and prints with popping, stringing and rough surfaces. See Symptoms of wet filament.
Wipe tower
See prime tower.

X

X2D
The Bambu Lab dual-nozzle printer this compendium focuses on, with a 300 °C nozzle and an actively heated chamber up to 65 °C. See Dual-nozzle printing.
XY compensation
A slicer setting that adjusts outer dimensions or hole sizes to offset shrinkage and bead width, improving fit. See Calibration.

Y

Yield strength
The stress at which a material starts to deform permanently. Beyond it, parts do not spring back.
Young’s modulus
A measure of stiffness: stress divided by strain in the elastic range. Higher means stiffer; PLA ≈ 3.5 GPa, TPU ≈ 0.01–0.1 GPa.

Z

Z banding
Regular horizontal bands on walls caused by Z-axis irregularities, such as a bent lead screw or inconsistent layer heights.
Z-hop
Lifting the nozzle slightly during travel moves to avoid dragging across printed parts. Can increase stringing.
Z offset
