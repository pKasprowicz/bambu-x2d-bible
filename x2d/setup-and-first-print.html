X2D setup & first print

From closed box to a finished first model: how to unpack and place the machine, power it, connect it, run the first-time routines, install the AMS 2 Pro, load filament, slice in Bambu Studio and watch your first print, with a checklist and the mistakes that trip most beginners.

Follow the official guide for the packaging details

Bambu Lab ships the X2D with packaging that protects moving parts during transport, and the official unboxing guide and quick-start guide in the box show exactly which pieces to remove. This page does not reproduce those instructions from memory: the research behind this compendium does not describe every transport lock and foam piece, so steps in that section are written generically and labeled typical. When our description and the printed guide disagree, the guide wins. Also see Your first print for the general method.

🔰 What is in the box

There are two ways to buy: the X2D alone, or the X2D Combo, which adds an AMS 2 Pro. The printer ships with a textured PEI plate on the bed, anti-vibration feet and a filament buffer. The combo’s AMS 2 Pro comes with its desiccant and the exhaust-fan bundle packed inside it.

📦 Unboxing and transport protection

The X2D weighs 16.25 kg and is 392 × 406 × 478 mm, so lift it with two hands, or with a helper, by the base, not by the door, the lid or the toolhead. Work on the table where it will live, because every move afterwards risks bending a rod or knocking a sensor.

  1. Open the carton and read the quick start guide. It shows the package layering and the removal order.
  2. Lift the printer out and set it on the intended table.
  3. Remove the packing foam, tape and any transport protection that the guide marks, inside the chamber and around the toolhead and the bed. Typical places to look are the toolhead, the build plate area and the door; follow the guide for the exact set.
  4. Check the chamber for loose items (the toolbox and spare parts are often inside) and remove them.
  5. Mount the touchscreen, the spool holder and the auxiliary extruder from the accessory box as shown in the guide. The auxiliary extruder goes on the rear panel; its PTFE tube is the 525 mm white one.
  6. Set the anti-vibration feet in place on the table before the final positioning.
Do not power on with packing still inside

Powering on with transport protection fitted can damage the motion system or cause calibration errors. If you are unsure whether a piece belongs to the packaging, check the guide or the Bambu Lab wiki before turning on the printer.

📍 Placement

Choose a stable, level, heat-resistant surface. The printer will shake a little at high speed, and a wobbly table amplifies that. Leave about 500 × 700 × 600 mm of free space as published by Bambu, and do not block the rear exhaust fan, the purge chute, the side intakes or the area above the AMS. Ambient conditions: 10–30 °C and humidity below 85%. Keep it away from direct sunlight and drafts (typical advice), and out of the room where you sleep.

🔌 Power and network

First check the label for the voltage version: 200–240 V (1600 W at 220 V) or 100–120 V (1100 W at 110 V). Use the supplied power cord in a grounded outlet; the printer is not meant for extension reels of unknown quality. Standby draws about 7–8 W, typical steady printing about 250 W for PLA and about 550 W for PC at 25 °C room temperature. If your circuit is weak, the Low Power setting caps power at about 750 W and stretches the bed warm-up.

Connectivity is by dual-band Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz). There is no wired Ethernet port. There are two operating styles:

☁️

Cloud mode

Bind the printer to your Bambu account (via a QR code in Bambu Handy or a PIN code in Studio 2.5.3.60 or later). You can then send jobs and watch from anywhere.

🏠

LAN Only mode

Keeps traffic in your local network. Enable LAN Only in the printer’s Settings; you connect using the access code shown there. Remote features that need the cloud are not available.

Binding to your account

  1. Power on the printer and follow the on-screen guide to select a Wi-Fi network and a language.
  2. Open Bambu Handy and sign in. Scan the QR code shown on the printer screen, or use the PIN-code route in Bambu Studio 2.5.3.60 or later.
  3. Name the printer and confirm the binding.
  4. Check the firmware in Settings. Update before your first print; the firmware has changed many times since launch, and updates include fixes for the AMS 2 Pro and spaghetti detection.
Send without a USB drive

Because jobs can be stored on the printer’s own storage, you can Send to built-in storage & print from Studio without any USB stick. A USB-A 2.0 port is still there for FAT32 or exFAT drives (one drive only; 32–64 GB is recommended). Without a drive, video recording and log export are not available, although internal timelapse storage is possible since firmware 01.01.00.00.

🚀 First-run guide and calibrations

On first power-on the screen guides you through a short Beginner’s Guide, and later you can reopen it from the Toolbox. The printer runs calibrations that it needs to know the exact geometry of itself. They live under Settings → Calibration and include:

  • Motor noise cancellation and vibration compensation: the printer measures its own resonances.
  • Auto bed leveling (and a high-temperature variant): maps the bed.
  • Nozzle offset calibration: an eddy-current sensor and the coil behind the bed measure the height difference between the two nozzles.
  • High-precision nozzle offset: the toolhead camera uses a dark and a light filament on the two nozzles to refine the X/Y offset. It is not possible with a filament track switch installed.
  • Live-view camera calibration: no longer needed since firmware 01.02.00.00.
  • First-layer calibration (advanced): fine-tune in steps of ±0.02–0.05 mm.
  • Motion accuracy calibration: needs the optional Vision Encoder.

🧵 Install the AMS 2 Pro and the buffer

The buffer comes installed on the printer. The AMS 2 Pro stands on top. The path is: AMS → PTFE tube → buffer → printer, and the AMS needs the 6-pin cable for data and power. Use the new cable that comes with the combo; the older cable gives unstable connections.

AMS Setup: assigning the unit to a nozzle

When the AMS is first detected, the screen opens AMS Setup. Choose Auto Mode-Detect (the Hall sensors determine which inlet you used; this requires filament in the AMS, nothing loaded in the printer and an empty buffer) or Manual, where you assign the AMS to Left or Right. Once filament has been sent through, the choice is locked. The recommended first combination is the AMS 2 Pro on the upper (left/main) inlet, because the main nozzle handles the widest range of materials.

📥 Load your first filament

Into the AMS

Seat the spool in a slot, with the end of the filament pointing to the slot’s inlet. Insert about 2 cm of filament; the feeder grabs it and the AMS reads the RFID tag. Bambu spools appear in the screen with the correct material and color. Add a third-party spool by setting its type and color manually. You do not need to push the filament all the way to the toolhead; the AMS loads it when a print calls for it.

Into the external spool holder

For a spool outside the AMS (for example TPU), edit the type and color on the screen, push the filament into the PTFE tube until it stops, tap Load and confirm when plastic leaves the nozzle. Check the extrusion: it should be a smooth, uniform line, not a clicking, curling or bubbling one.

💻 Bambu Studio on your computer

Bambu Studio is Bambu’s slicer (see the slicers overview for the concepts). Install it from Bambu’s site, make sure it is at least version 2.5.3.60 if you want PIN-code binding or the high-flow hotends, and sign in with the same account as the printer. In the printer selection, pick the X2D and the nozzle diameter that is installed (0.4 mm hardened steel to start). Open the Device tab to see the printer, camera and AMS status, and sync the AMS filaments so the slicer’s filament list matches your slots.

  1. Choose the printer and nozzle (left and right nozzle diameters are the same; mixed sizes are not yet supported for dual-nozzle printing).
  2. Sync the filaments from the AMS. Colors and types of RFID spools appear automatically.
  3. Choose a plate: the textured PEI plate matches the printer; Studio warns about a mismatch.
  4. Pick a profile for the material, usually the Bambu profile for the filament you loaded.

🖨️ Your first print

The simplest first print is one that is already on the printer. The built-in models are pre-sliced for the left (primary) hotend only, so they do not need either Studio or the AMS to be perfect. Bambu’s own guide has you print a scraper this way. It tests the plate, the heater, the extrusion and the camera at the same time, and the scraper is useful afterwards.

Option 1 · From the touchscreen

  1. Prepare the plate. Check that the bed and plate are clean and free of debris. If you touched the surface, wash the plate with warm water and dish soap and let it dry.
  2. Open Print Files and choose a built-in model.
  3. Confirm the plate type, the nozzle, the timelapse and other options, and match the filament to the left hotend.
  4. Start. The printer heats, runs its automatic leveling and calibration moves, purges a line and starts printing.

Option 2 · From Bambu Studio

  1. Add a model (a calibration cube from MakerWorld or one from your own files) and place it near the middle front of the plate.
  2. Slice the plate, review the preview and the filament assignment (a single material goes to the left nozzle by default).
  3. Print Plate or Send, then confirm on the printer.

Option 3 · From Bambu Handy

Browse MakerWorld or your library, tap Print, choose the plate and filament, and start. Handy is also the tool you use afterwards to watch from the sofa.

📱 Reading the touchscreen

The 5-inch screen has a left-hand menu with five items: Homepage, Controls, Filaments, Settings and HMS (Health Management System, the place where warnings and errors appear). The Files tab lists Internal, History and External storage. Under Controls you find Air Condition, Speed, Motion (including Free Move), Nozzle & Extruder, temperatures, and the chamber light.

👀 Monitoring: app, camera and AI

The live-view camera (1080p, 30 fps) is in a corner of the chamber. Turn the chamber light on, because spaghetti detection needs it. You can see the picture in Bambu Handy or in Studio’s Device tab. Detections you will meet include spaghetti, purge-chute pile-up, nozzle clumping, part displacement, foreign objects on the plate and a wrong plate. Each can be set to High, Medium or Low sensitivity; Low is common for long overnight prints. The camera lens must be clean: a dirty lens triggers a hygiene warning.

Timelapse

Internal timelapse storage is supported since firmware 01.01.00.00, so a USB drive is not required. A drive is still useful for exporting logs when you need help.

🧹 Finishing and removal

  1. Let the bed cool to room temperature. Parts release more easily and are less likely to bend.
  2. Remove the plate and flex it gently; the part comes off. Do not pry with a sharp blade against the plate.
  3. Scrape the pre-extruded purge line from the plate edge and put it in the bin.
  4. Check the AMS: after a finished job it retracts the filament automatically; after a canceled job, unload manually from the screen.
  5. Peel off the rest (brim, supports), and clean the plate if needed.

🔰 Good habits from the first week

Most of the long-term reliability of a printer comes from a few routines that are easy to start on day one. None of them take more than a minute.

  • Look at the first layer every time you change material or plate. The first layer is where adhesion, nozzle offset and plate cleanliness show up; a quick look saves a failed overnight job.
  • Keep spools sealed or in the AMS. Moisture is the most common hidden cause of poor surfaces. The AMS 2 Pro’s sealed design and humidity sensor help, and the desiccant color tells you when it is saturated (see the AMS 2 Pro page).
  • Do not block the printer’s air paths. Bambu asks for free space around the machine (a 500 × 700 × 600 mm envelope in the guide) and warns against covering the rear exhaust fan, the purge chute, the side intakes and the area above the AMS.
  • Mind the hot parts. The nozzle reaches up to 300 °C, the bed up to 120 °C and the chamber up to 65 °C. Wait for the bed to cool before you touch the plate, and open the door only when you must during a hot-chamber print; the printer has Open Door Detection and will react.
  • Let the printer run its calibrations. Motor-noise cancellation, vibration compensation and automatic bed leveling exist for a reason. Re-run them after maintenance that touches the motion system, as described on the maintenance page.
  • Keep the firmware current. Bambu’s release notes show real changes between versions (for example internal timelapse storage and print-while-drying arrived in 01.01.00.00), so check the version before you ask for help.
If something looks wrong on day one

Stop the print, open the HMS page on the screen and read the message; it normally names the problem and a fix. Photograph the screen before you power-cycle, and note the firmware version. If the cause is unclear, Bambu’s wiki and support channels are the authoritative sources, and the troubleshooting chapter gives a general method.

✅ Day-1 checklist

  • Voltage version matches my outlet; power cord is seated.
  • All packing foam, tape and transport protection removed (as per the guide).
  • Printer stands on a stable table with free space and a clear rear exhaust.
  • Wi-Fi connected, printer bound to my account (or LAN Only set up).
  • Firmware updated and Bambu Studio is up to date.
  • Touchscreen, spool holder and auxiliary extruder mounted.
  • AMS 2 Pro placed, 6-pin cable plugged in, PTFE from AMS to buffer upper inlet.
  • Desiccant removed from its bag and installed.
  • AMS Setup completed; spools loaded and recognized.
  • Plate clean; first print from the built-in model finished.
  • I know where the HMS menu is and how to unload filament.
  • Spare hotend, cutters and tools located in the toolbox.

🚫 Most common beginner mistakes

Forgetting the packaging

Powering on with a piece of foam or transport protection still fitted. Always check against the guide.

Using a dirty plate

Fingerprints and dust make adhesion poor. Wash the plate with warm water and dish soap when it gets greasy.

Old 6-pin cable

The older cable is unstable with the AMS 2 Pro. Use the new one, and plug it into the buffer’s port.

Wrong filament type

Choosing PLA in the slicer for a PETG spool. The profile selects temperatures, cooling and flow.

Mixing nozzle sizes

Dual-nozzle printing needs the same diameter on both sides, which also applies after you swap one hotend.

Blocking the exhaust

Pushing the printer against a wall or shelf. The chamber cannot breathe and the fan noise rises.

Skipping the first layer

Leaving the room during the first layer of a new material. Almost all early failures start there.

TPU through the AMS

Putting flexible filament in an AMS slot. Use the external spool path; see TPU & flexibles.

✅ Key takeaways

  • Follow the guide in the box for unpacking; this compendium only gives the general method.
  • Check the voltage version, leave free space, keep the exhaust clear and use 10–30 °C ambient conditions.
  • Bind the printer through Bambu Handy (QR) or Studio (PIN) for cloud use, or choose LAN Only.
  • Install the AMS 2 Pro with the new 6-pin cable and the PTFE to the buffer’s upper inlet, then run AMS Setup.
  • The easiest first print is a built-in, pre-sliced model for the left nozzle.
  • Watch the first layer; let the plate cool; flex it to release the part.
