X2D maintenance & troubleshooting

A dual-nozzle printer with a heated chamber and a remote extruder has more parts to care for than a simple single-nozzle machine. This page gives a practical maintenance schedule, explains the X2D's error classes and the HMS system, and walks through step-by-step recoveries with flowcharts.

What you will learn

What to inspect and when, how to lubricate rods and lead screws and recalibrate afterward, how to deal with nozzle blobs and clogs, how the auxiliary extruder's PTFE and locking nut work, how to read AMS LED states, what HMS is, what to do about each class of error, how to prepare logs for support, and how to move or store the printer.

Generic theory lives elsewhere

This page is X2D-specific. For general principles of print failures read Troubleshooting, and for general upkeep of any printer see Maintenance. Intervals and procedures here follow Bambu's published X2D maintenance documentation; always power off and unplug before touching mechanics.

1. Principles

  • Power off and unplug before maintenance on motion, extruders and electronics. Let the hotend and heatbed cool unless a procedure explicitly says to heat them.
  • Keep a log. A simple note of date, what was done and the printer's behavior afterward turns vague symptoms into patterns (see exercise X-12).
  • Recalibrate after mechanical work. After lubrication, moving the printer or heatbed work, run the full calibration set: motor noise cancellation, vibration compensation and auto bed leveling.
  • Prevent first, repair second. Most failures trace back to dirt, moisture or worn consumables.
Tool kit

Bambu's documentation lists lube oil and grease, non-woven cloth, isopropyl alcohol (IPA), a brush, tweezers and the supplied tools (Allen keys H1.5 and H2.0, an 8 mm wrench, an unclogging pin, scraper blade, spare hotend and sock, two PTFE tubes and desiccant). Add heat-resistant gloves for hotend work.

3. Mechanics: rods, screws and belts

Lubrication (rods and lead screws)

  1. Power off and unplug.Move the toolhead by hand to expose the rods.
  2. Wipe the rods with IPAand a non-woven cloth to remove old oil and dust.
  3. Apply oil.Add 1 to 2 drops per 5 cm on the X and Y rods. Lead screws take grease, not oil.
  4. Spread it.Move the toolhead slowly 3 to 5 times across the full travel.
  5. Recalibrate.Run motor noise cancellation, vibration compensation and auto bed leveling.

Belt tension

The X2D has a semi-automatic belt tensioner. Bambu names three symptoms of low tension: circular holes printed out of round (elliptical), lost steps (layer shifts) and a "resonance frequency too low" message during vibration compensation. If you see them, follow the on-screen or wiki tensioning procedure and re-run vibration compensation.

Chamber and fans

Wipe the chamber floor and walls, and clean the cooling fans on both sides, using a brush and compressed air. Debris on fans reduces airflow, which reduces chamber control and filtration. Do this more often after ABS, ASA or nylon prints.

4. Nozzles, the gear-and-trigger switch and the flow blocker

The X2D switches nozzles with a Gear-and-Trigger mechanism: a lever and push block moved by the toolhead's own travel, so there is no separate motor to fail. A flow blocker shuts the idle nozzle, preventing oozing; when you print on the auxiliary nozzle the toolhead moves the flow blocker under the main hotend, and the aux hotend lowers, which costs about 4 mm of build height. Filament cutters sit on both sides, triggered by a stopper that pushes the lever mid-chamber.

  • Keep the lever path clear. Stringy residue or a loose cable can stop a switch.
  • Nozzle offset calibration uses an eddy-current sensor and a coil behind the plate; the optional High-Precision Nozzle Offset Calibration uses the toolhead camera with a dark and a light filament (not available while a Filament Track Switch is fitted).
  • After reinstalling a hotend, recalibrate the nozzle offset. The hotends are interchangeable left to right; swapping them is also a clean diagnostic trick.
  • The hotend sock has three yellow markers used by AI detection; keep it clean. The toolhead front cover is magnetic and cover-presence is detected since firmware 01.01.00.00.

Cleaning a blob on the hotend

  1. Put on heat-resistant gloves.The nozzle will be hot.
  2. Lower the bed (do not use Home)and enable Heating Base Maintenance Mode from the toolbox.
  3. Heat the nozzle slightly above the printing temperature of the last material (the guide gives PLA at about 250 °C as an example) and pull the blob away with tweezers.
  4. For deeper cleaning lower the temperature in steps (to about 200 °C, then 150 °C) as the guide describes, and clean the sock.
  5. Recalibrate the nozzle offset if you reinstalled the hotend.

Aux extruder PTFE and locking nut

The right extruder feeds filament through a white 525 mm PTFE tube secured at the toolhead with a locking nut. When you tighten the nut a washer makes a "click" to confirm. If, with the nut tight, the tube can still move up and down, the tube end is worn and should be replaced; typical symptoms are stringing and poor retraction. Use the 8 mm wrench on the nut. The gray 555 mm PTFE connects the chamber inlet to the main hotend.

5. Filters, cameras, plates and wipers

  • Carbon filter: replace when heavily contaminated. The cover can be rinsed but must be completely dry before reinstalling. The G3 and H12 HEPA elements follow the same logic: look at them, do not guess.
  • Live-view camera lens: clean about every 6 months, more often if you print ABS. The printer also runs a camera dirt and obstruction check at the start of every print.
  • Textured PEI plate: both sides are usable; do not touch the print surface; wash with warm water and dish soap. Do not use glue with PLA on textured PEI.
  • Purge wiper: a rough wipe plus a fine wipe on the plate; the silicone wiper is a consumable, replace when damaged.
  • Cutter blade: check every 8–12 spools (regular filament) or every 6–10 spools (CF/GF/PPA). If the AMS often fails to unload, suspect a dull blade first.

6. AMS 2 Pro care

  • Desiccant: yellow is fresh, purple is saturated. Replace after about 4–6 weeks of continuous use or as soon as it is purple.
  • Spools: width 50–68 mm, diameter 197–202 mm. Plastic spools are preferred; cardboard spools need adapter rings.
  • PTFE tubes: inspect; replace on feeding errors or after heavy use of abrasive filament. Third-party CF/GF filament may wear tubes faster than the tuned official ones.
  • Pneumatic couplers, ceramic funnels, support shaft, hub: check at spool changes and on feeding problems; the hub has magnets and Hall sensors that dust can disturb.
  • Power for drying: a printer-powered AMS dries with reduced power during printing; the official 24 V / 4 A adapter restores full power. Drying needs the 6-pin connection (no offline drying).
  • Do not dry mixed temperature classes in one AMS together.

7. Error classes and the HMS concept

What is HMS?

HMS stands for Health Management System. It is Bambu's way of turning sensor readings and printer events into a coded message: the printer notices something (a sensor trips, a motor misses steps, a filament runs out, the camera sees spaghetti), assigns the event a code and a severity, shows it on the screen under the HMS tab, pushes it to Bambu Handy and Studio, and links it to a wiki article that explains the cause and the steps to fix it.

Using HMS well

Read the message text first; the code is mainly there so you and support can refer to the same thing. Note the code and the time, and when contacting support include them. This page deliberately does not list individual code numbers: look them up in the Assistant on the printer or in Bambu's wiki, where they match your firmware.

Error classes on the X2D

AMS errors

Feed failures, filament broken in path, tangles, drying faults. Usually mechanical (tube, coupler, spool) or moisture-related.

Nozzle switching

The toolhead cannot change between nozzles, or an abnormal toolhead change is detected (a detection added in firmware 01.01.01.00). Mechanical: lever, flow blocker, obstacles.

Clogs and flow

Hotend clog, extruder clog, partial clogs that cause under-extrusion; blobs on the nozzle.

AI detection

Spaghetti, purge-chute pile-up, nozzle clumping, foreign objects, part displacement, plate-type mismatch. Each has a sensitivity setting.

Motion and calibration

Step loss, resonance warnings, belt tension, leveling failures.

Environment and power

Open-door detection, idle-heating protection (5 minutes), chamber over-temperature, power loss during drying.

8. Step-by-step recoveries

"Filament broken in path" (AMS)

Brittle, moist filament is a common cause. Disconnect the white PTFE tubes, remove the broken piece (the hub and the tube ends are where it usually hides), reconnect, Re-read the slot, and dry the spool before retrying.

Clog diagnosis: hotend or extruder?

Bambu's guide uses an unload test. If the filament can be retracted, the clog is most likely at the hotend; verify by extruding with the hotend removed. If it cannot retract, suspect the extruder; swap the other side's hotend in to compare. Both can be clogged, but that is rare.

AMS feed or unload failure flowchart

Filament tangle and extruder resistance

The main (left) extruder is a servo extruder that senses resistance, which is how the X2D detects tangles. If a spool tangles: stop, free the loose turns, make sure the filament leaves the spool toward the AMS in the right direction and check there is nothing catching on the spool edge. Reset the spool in the AMS and re-read it. The aux path has two sensors (aux extruder and toolhead right) that help locate where in the PTFE path a jam sits.

Nozzle switching problems

  1. Stop and look.Use the camera or open the door when safe to see whether the toolhead is physically stuck.
  2. Check the lever path and flow blockerfor filament strings, a blob or a foreign object.
  3. Clean the nozzle sock and wiper area.
  4. Re-run nozzle offset calibrationif cleaning does not help; use high-precision only with the track switch removed.
  5. Update firmwareif you are behind: 01.01.01.00 added abnormal-toolhead-change detection and aux loading fixes.

First layer too thin or uneven

Fiber-filled filament and nozzle blobs

Never cold pull with fiber-reinforced filament, and never use a metal tool on a hot nozzle sock near wiring. When in doubt, cool down, take a photo and ask support.

9. When to contact support, and logs

Contact Bambu support when: a part is physically broken, a safety feature trips repeatedly, an error returns after the documented fix, or a calibration cannot finish. Do not wait if you smell burning or see damage to a heater, wire or connector; power off and unplug first.

  • Export logs from the printer's settings to external storage. This requires a USB drive; without one you cannot export logs.
  • Record the code and time from the HMS tab, plus the plate, material and slicer version.
  • Take photos of the problem area before cleaning it.

10. Shipping, relocation and long-term storage

Moving the printer

The X2D weighs 16.25 kg and measures 392 × 406 × 478 mm. Plan the destination: Bambu recommends a space of about 500 × 700 × 600 mm for operation, ambient temperature of 10–30 °C and humidity below 85 %. After any move, re-run auto bed leveling (and the full calibration set after a long trip).

Shipping

Remove spools and unload the AMS; keep the original packaging and protective parts if possible; secure the toolhead as in the unboxing instructions. Photograph the printer before shipping. These are typical practices, not a Bambu-published list.

Long-term storage

Unload filament, empty the AMS and store spools sealed with desiccant. Keep the printer clean and dry, covered from dust. After storage re-run leveling and check the desiccant and filters.

Heatbed work

If anything touched the heatbed, re-run leveling. If you ever see odd bed height maps, use the first-layer troubleshooting flow above.

Key takeaways

  • Lubricate on a schedule tied to daily print hours, and recalibrate after.
  • Know the aux PTFE nut test and the unload test for clogs.
  • HMS codes are lookup keys: read the message, look up the code, note it for support.
  • AMS red LED patterns point to feeder, hub or tube problems; dry spools and free filament mechanically.
  • Export logs (needs USB), photograph problems, and escalate step by step.
  • Recalibrate after moving the printer, long storage or any heatbed or hotend work.
