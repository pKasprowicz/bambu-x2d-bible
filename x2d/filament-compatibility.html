Filament Compatibility: What Runs Where

The X2D has two nozzles with different feed paths, an AMS 2 Pro with its own rules and a heated chamber with two airflow modes. This page turns Bambu’s compatibility tables into something you can scan, and adds the reasoning behind them so you can make sensible calls for filaments the tables don’t list.

Bambu X2DIntermediate⏱ 24 min read
Read this before trusting any table

Compatibility is a moving target: Bambu adds materials, changes ratings between firmware and slicer versions, and publishes the authoritative list on its wiki. The ratings below are condensed from the Bambu wiki’s filament compatibility pages as available when this compendium was written. Cells where the source was unclear are marked Check. If a rating here disagrees with the wiki, the wiki wins. Items labeled “typical” are general 3D-printing practice, not Bambu specifications.

🔰 How to read the ratings

Bambu uses four symbols in its tables: available not recommended highly not recommended unavailable. “Not recommended” means it may work but quality or reliability suffers; “highly not recommended” means the combination is likely to cause failures or damage; “unavailable” means the filament (or the nozzle size for it) isn’t offered or isn’t supported. This page uses the same four levels plus check for uncertainty.

🔰 Nozzles: sizes and materials

The X2D ships with a 0.4 mm hardened-steel hotend installed and a spare in the toolbox. Because both nozzles can be swapped, the hotends are identical (300 °C maximum, quick-release lever, ceramic base with heater and thermistor, no terminals). Bambu lists these nozzle options:

Both nozzles, same diameter

Dual-nozzle printing requires the same diameter on both sides. If you want 0.6 mm, plan to buy two. The compatibility matrix below is therefore read per diameter: a filament that works at 0.4 mm may be unavailable at 0.2 mm (typically the fiber-filled and special-effect ones), and a few filaments are better at larger diameters.

Why hardened steel matters

Carbon-fiber and glass-fiber filaments act like fine sandpaper on a brass nozzle; they wear the orifice, so the line gets wider and detail degrades. The X2D’s pre-installed nozzle is hardened steel, which is why Bambu lists fiber-filled filaments as available on it. Hardened steel conducts heat a little less well than brass, which is why high-temperature and flow limits matter, but you do not need to swap nozzles to print most abrasive materials. Even so, nozzles are consumables: when the printed line gets visibly wider than expected, or a part loses its edges, replace the hotend.

  • Abrasive: carbon-fiber (CF) and glass-fiber (GF) filled filaments, glow-in-the-dark and some metal- or stone-filled filaments (typical).
  • Non-abrasive: plain PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU, most unfilled nylons and polycarbonates.
  • Check cutters: Bambu advises checking the filament cutter blades more often with CF/GF: every 6–10 rolls compared with 8–12 for ordinary filament.
  • Check the AMS path: avoid third-party hard, brittle fiber filaments in the AMS; they can break in the feeder.

🔧 The matrix: main (left) nozzle

The main nozzle with its direct-drive servo extruder accepts the broadest range. For the PLA family everything is available at 0.4 mm and above. The 0.2 mm diameter is unavailable for Marble, Sparkle, Wood, Glow, PLA-CF and PLA Aero. PLA Silk and PLA Aero are not recommended at the larger diameters (Silk at 0.6 and 0.8 mm; Aero at 0.6 and 0.8 mm). PETG is available, and PETG-CF is unavailable at 0.2 mm. ABS, ASA, ABS-GF and PC are available from 0.4 mm. TPU for AMS is available; the softer TPU 85A is unavailable at 0.4 mm and available at 0.6 and 0.8 mm, while TPU 90A and 95A are available. Support materials are available. The fiber-reinforced nylons and polyesters (PAHT-CF, PA6-GF, PET-CF, PA6-CF, PPA-CF) are available from 0.4 mm; PPS-CF is unavailable on all sizes.

🔧 The matrix: auxiliary (right) nozzle

The auxiliary nozzle is more restricted, because of the longer path, the stepper extruder and its slower motion limits. As Bambu documents it: PLA basic-type filaments are available from 0.4 mm; PLA Silk is highly not recommended at every size, though Silk+ is available at 0.4 mm; PLA-CF is mostly available; PLA Aero is unavailable. PETG Basic and PETG HF are not recommended and PETG-CF is highly not recommended. ABS is not recommended, ABS-GF is available, ASA is not recommended and ASA-CF is highly not recommended. PC is available from 0.4 mm. TPU for AMS is highly not recommended and all other TPU is unavailable. Support for ABS, PLA/PETG, PLA and PVA are available while Support for PA/PET is highly not recommended. PAHT-CF and PA6-GF are available, PET-CF is not recommended, PA6-CF is highly not recommended, and PPA-CF and PPS-CF are unavailable.

Bambu’s “recommended on aux” shortlist

Bambu’s own guidance for the auxiliary nozzle is to prefer official PLA (not Aero), ABS, ASA, supports, PC, PET and PA, and to be careful with PLA Silk, PETG, PETG-CF, ASA-CF, PA6-CF and Support for PA/PET. You may notice it overlaps imperfectly with the matrix cells above: ABS and ASA appear in the shortlist but are flagged “not recommended” in some matrix columns. This is exactly why you should check the wiki’s current table and the filament’s own page before committing a long job.

🔧 What the track switch changes

The optional Filament Track Switch (sold separately; firmware 01.01.00.00 or later, Studio 2.5.3 or later, Handy 3.18.0 or later) lets a single AMS feed either nozzle and enables dynamic mapping. It has its own compatibility rating because filament must travel through an extra junction and the PTFE path is longer. Per Bambu’s table, it is rated available for PLA basics, Matte, Lite, Gradient, Metal, Translucent, Tough+, Marble, Sparkle, Wood and Galaxy; PETG including PETG-CF; ABS; ASA; PC FR; Support for PLA, PETG, PA/PET; PVA; PAHT-CF; PA6-GF. It is rated not recommended for PLA Glow, Silk/Silk+, PLA-CF and Support for ABS; highly not recommended for PLA Aero, ABS-GF, ASA Aero, ASA-CF, PC and TPU for AMS; and unavailable for other TPU, PET-CF, PA6-CF, PPA-CF and PPS-CF. If your multi-material plan relies on the track switch, check that every filament in the plan is rated for it.

🔧 Chamber behavior by material class

The chamber has two airflow modes. In cooling mode (for PLA and TPU) the auxiliary part-cooling fans run, the right fan draws outside air and the rear fan exhausts. In heating mode (ABS, ASA, PC, PA) the left heating fan and right filtration fan recirculate air through the filter, and the chamber can be actively heated up to 65 °C. In heating mode the bed lowers to about Z160 to make room. Preheating tip from Bambu: set the bed to at least 90 °C and wait about 30 minutes to get the chamber over 50 °C.

Open the lid for low-temperature materials?

For PLA and TPU, Bambu’s guidance is to run in cooling mode and not to heat the chamber. Heating the chamber for such filaments can soften them and cause feed problems. Do not use heating mode for TPU.

🔧 TPU and other flexibles: the path limits

TPU is the compatibility exception that surprises people most. On the X2D, TPU is supported on the main (left) nozzle only. In the AMS 2 Pro, the guidance is not to run TPU/TPE through the unit (the AMS page explains why). Instead:

  • External spool. Mount the spool on the top holder and feed it through a short PTFE tube (about 175 mm) into the left inlet. This is the direct path Bambu describes.
  • TPU Feed Assist Module. An accessory path in which an AMS HT is used as dryer/feeder and a TPU bypass port leads filament to the main nozzle.
  • Settings. Bambu suggests about 225 °C nozzle for TPU 85A/90A, bed 30–35 °C, no chamber heating, keep the default volumetric speed limit, flow dynamics set to Auto or Off, and put the model mid-front on the plate. Do not use glue on the textured PEI plate with TPU.
  • Storage. Keep TPU under 20% relative humidity. Dry it for 8 hours at 70 °C in an oven, or at 75–85 °C on the heatbed.
  • Cold pull. Perform a cold pull before TPU to clear residue, but never when the previous filament was fiber-filled.

🔧 PVA, supports and dissolvables

Support filaments are available on both nozzles in Bambu’s tables, with the exception of Support for PA/PET on the aux nozzle (highly not recommended). Plain PVA is hygroscopic: it absorbs moisture quickly, becomes brittle and bubbles. Bambu’s AMS guidance is to avoid absorbent PVA in the AMS; dry it thoroughly, store it sealed and use an external spool or a drying-capable feeder when you need it. Pair the support with the model material: Support for ABS with ABS, Support for PLA/PETG with those materials, and Support for PA/PET with nylon or PET-based filaments. A mismatched support may bond too strongly or not at all. When you place the support on the auxiliary nozzle, remember its speed limit; it is good for interface layers but slower for bulk.

🔧 Drying guidance per class

Wet filament pops, strings, bubbles and loses strength. The AMS 2 Pro has two drying modules with a maximum of 65 °C (only if ambient is 25 °C or higher), and you should never dry high-temperature filament together with low-temperature spools. The numbers in the figure below are typical values used widely in the hobby; Bambu does not publish one master per-material table in the sources used here, and the spool label or the wiki always overrides them. Only the TPU values are from Bambu’s TPU guide.

  • Rotating drying (the AMS turns the spool 30° every 5 minutes) is the preferred mode. Static drying is not suitable for PLA, PVA, Support for PLA/PETG and TPU.
  • The yellow-to-purple color-changing desiccant (replace or regenerate about every 4–6 weeks) shows when the AMS interior is humid.
  • Fiber-filled nylons and PC dry best in an external dryer; they exceed the AMS’s 65 °C ceiling.

🔧 Bambu RFID spools vs third-party spools

Bambu spools carry an RFID tag. When you insert a spool into an AMS slot, the two RFID coils read it and the printer fills in the material, color and often the recommended settings. Third-party spools have no tag; you set the type and color on the screen (or in the slicer), and choose a matching profile. Third-party spools must also physically fit: width 50–68 mm, outer diameter 197–202 mm.

Automatic refill (when a spool runs out and another takes over) only works with an identical brand, type and color on the same nozzle side.

🔧 Creating a custom filament profile

A profile in Bambu Studio is a named set of filament parameters: nozzle temperature, bed temperature, flow ratio, cooling, retraction, flow dynamics and so on. For unfamiliar filaments, start from the closest Bambu profile, then tune it deliberately. For the general method, see settings and profiles and the calibration chapter.

🚀 A repeatable checking routine

Because the tables change, a short routine beats memorizing them. Before any job that uses an unfamiliar filament, nozzle or accessory, run through this list:

  1. Identify the exact filament. Brand, product line and variant matter: “PLA Silk” and “PLA Silk+” are rated differently, and “PETG” differs from “PETG-CF”.
  2. Find its row on the wiki. Read the columns for your nozzle diameter on the main nozzle, the aux nozzle and, if you use it, the track switch.
  3. Check the path. Is it going through the AMS (avoid TPU/TPE, absorbent PVA and brittle third-party fibers) or from the external spool?
  4. Check the nozzle. Abrasive fillers need hardened steel, which the pre-installed hotend is; the diameter must match on both sides for dual-nozzle jobs.
  5. Check the chamber. Cooling mode for PLA/TPU; heating mode for ABS/ASA/PC/PA, and preheat with the bed at 90 °C or more for about 30 minutes to get the chamber past 50 °C.
  6. Dry it. If the spool is old, opened or from a humid room, dry it as the label says before you assume the profile is at fault.
  7. Test small. A 20-minute test print tells you more than an hour of reading.

This is also the logic behind the “Check” cells in the heat map: they are not failures of the table, but reminders that some combinations depend on details that only the current wiki entry can settle.

✅ Key takeaways

  • Check Bambu’s wiki for the exact filament, nozzle size, nozzle side and accessory (AMS, track switch) before a long job.
  • The main nozzle accepts the widest list; the aux nozzle is more restrictive, and TPU is main-only.
  • The X2D ships with a hardened-steel 0.4 mm hotend; dual-nozzle jobs need the same diameter on both sides.
  • Cooling mode for PLA/TPU; heating mode for ABS/ASA/PC/PA; the chamber reaches up to 65 °C.
  • The AMS 2 Pro dries to 65 °C at most; use a separate dryer for hotter-drying materials; keep TPU out of the AMS.
  • RFID presets save time; third-party spools need manual type, color and a tested profile.
  • Keep separate presets for the aux nozzle because its flow dynamics are calibrated manually.
  • When Bambu says “not recommended”, treat it as a real constraint, not a suggestion to override.
  • For a two-filament job, pass three gates: each filament allowed on its nozzle, one chamber mode for both, and a joint that bonds or locks; otherwise print two jobs.
