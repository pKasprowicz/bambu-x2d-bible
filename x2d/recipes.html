Part 6 · Bambu X2D + AMS 2 Pro

Print recipes for the X2D + AMS 2 Pro

Fifteen complete jobs, each with a goal, filament and slot plan, nozzle assignment, key slicer settings, orientation, a qualitative time class, pitfalls and finishing. Every number is a starting point: your spool, plate and room will move it.

IntermediateX2D + AMS 2 Pro⏱ 40 min read
How to use this page

Pick the recipe closest to your goal, read the slot-plan figure, then adapt the settings with your own filament presets (see settings and profiles). Material behavior is explained in engineering and chamber and TPU and flexibles.

Slot-plan assumptions

Bambu pairs each AMS with one nozzle side: an AMS on the buffer's upper inlet feeds the left (main) hotend, one on the lower inlet feeds the right (auxiliary) hotend. A single AMS 2 Pro, as in most households, therefore feeds the left nozzle. Recipes that use the right nozzle assume a second AMS on the lower inlet (an AMS HT or another AMS 2 Pro) or a Filament Track Switch (sold separately; Studio 2.5.3+, firmware 01.01.00.00+). The right nozzle is limited to 200 mm/s and about 1000 mm/s², and TPU never goes there. If you only own one AMS, use the "single-AMS note" in each recipe.

Recipe 1 · Multi-color figurine

  • GoalA multi-color character figurine with a clean surface and minimal waste.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A: slot 1 body color, 2 accent, 3 eye/detail, 4 base. AMS B slot 1: Support for PLA (right nozzle).
  • Nozzle assignmentColors → left nozzle (Filament-saving or Convenience grouping); support → right nozzle. Single-AMS note: put the support in slot 4 and drop one color.
  • Key settings (starting points)0.4 mm nozzle, layer 0.08–0.12 mm, 2–3 walls, 10–15 % infill, prime tower on, tree supports, outer wall slow.
  • OrientationFace up and forward, so color transitions land on hidden areas.
  • Time classLong (hours), driven by layer count and color changes.
  • PitfallsColor bleed after dark→light changes (check the flush matrix), tower wobble, visible support scars on the face.
  • FinishingTrim supports, light sanding on seams, matte clear coat if wanted.

Recipe 2 · Functional PETG bracket

  • GoalA bracket that carries a static load, e.g. a shelf or monitor arm mount.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: PETG. Others unused (or a second PETG as an Auto-Refill backup: same brand, type and color, same side).
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle only. Right nozzle idle.
  • Key settings (starting points)0.4 or 0.6 mm nozzle, layer 0.20–0.28 mm, 4+ walls, 30–40 % gyroid infill, bed typically 70–80 °C, textured PEI.
  • OrientationLayers perpendicular to the load direction, so the load is not trying to split layer bonds; the long edge on the plate. See strength and orientation.
  • Time classMedium.
  • PitfallsStringing from wet PETG, screws crushing into holes (add washers or inserts), layer splitting if loaded across layers.
  • FinishingDeburr, test-fit screws, add 3–4 perimeters around holes with a modifier.

Recipe 3 · ASA outdoor part

  • GoalA UV-resistant part for outdoors, e.g. a sensor housing or garden clip.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: ASA, dried if the spool has been open for a while.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle. (The right nozzle is rated for ASA with caution.)
  • Key settings (starting points)Bed 100–110 °C (documented range for ABS/PC; check the ASA spool label), air mode Heating, high-temperature bed leveling first, full pre-heat soak, brim, low part fan.
  • OrientationFlat on the plate; round the footprint corners.
  • Time classMedium to long, with soak.
  • PitfallsOpening the door early (cracks), fumes (filter in), corner lift on large footprints.
  • FinishingWait until the chamber has cooled; optional vapor smoothing is a post-processing step (see post-processing).

Recipe 4 · PA-CF jig

  • GoalA stiff, wear-resistant assembly jig or fixture.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: PAHT-CF or PA6-CF (dried). Keep high-temperature spools away from low-temperature ones during drying.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle only: PA6-CF is rated not recommended on the right nozzle. Standard 0.4 mm or larger hardened-steel hotend (0.2 mm is not available for these filaments).
  • Key settings (starting points)Air mode Heating, high-temperature bed leveling, engineering plate with glue, brim, moderate volumetric speed.
  • OrientationOrient reference faces down; let functional faces be bottom-surface smooth.
  • Time classMedium to long.
  • PitfallsWet filament (rough, weak), AMS tube wear with third-party CF, warp.
  • FinishingLight sanding of locating faces; test with real parts before using.

Recipe 5 · TPU gasket + rigid housing

  • GoalA rigid PETG housing with a TPU gasket that seals a lid.
  • Filament and slotsExternal spool: TPU 95A HF (recommended external path). AMS B slot 1 (or A if wiring allows): PETG for the housing.
  • Nozzle assignmentTPU → left nozzle only (direct path); PETG → right nozzle via the lower-inlet AMS, with a 200 mm/s cap. Single-AMS note: print the housing and the gasket as two jobs, TPU alone on the left nozzle.
  • Key settings (starting points)TPU: 95A HF on 0.4–0.8 mm, bed 30–35 °C, no chamber heating, default volumetric limit, flow dynamics Auto/Off, glue only on smooth/engineering plates (never on textured PEI), model at the middle-front. Gasket: print flat, 100 % or high wall count, design for modest compression.
  • OrientationGasket flat; housing with its sealing face either on the plate or downhill-protected from support scars.
  • Time classMedium; TPU part is slow per mm³.
  • PitfallsFeeding TPU from a standard AMS path, TPU on the right nozzle (unsupported), glue on textured PEI.
  • FinishingTrim stringing with a sharp blade; leak-test with water before relying on it.

Recipe 6 · PLA/PETG support interface

  • GoalA PLA part with overhangs that release cleanly from a PETG support interface (PETG and PLA bond poorly, so the interface peels away).
  • Filament and slotsAMS A: slot 1 PLA (part), slot 2 PETG (interface layers only).
  • Nozzle assignmentBoth on the left nozzle (single AMS works). With a second AMS you may move the PETG interface to the right nozzle, accepting its speed cap.
  • Key settings (starting points)Support filament: PETG for the interface only; support body can stay in the part filament or the same PETG; top Z-distance about 0.1–0.2 mm; 2–3 interface layers; interface spacing 0 for a dense face; prime tower on and sized for the PLA↔PETG flush.
  • OrientationPlace the most visible overhang so that the interface covers the smallest area.
  • Time classLonger than single-material, because of the extra changes and flush.
  • PitfallsPLA and PETG have different temperature windows: a too-short flush leaves PETG in the PLA nozzle and vice versa; interface too thin tears; PETG too cold bonds weakly to the support body.
  • FinishingPeel supports along the interface; any fuzz comes off with a deburring tool.

Recipe 7 · Lithophane

  • GoalA backlit photo panel where thickness encodes brightness.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: white or natural PLA. All values below are typical, not X2D-documented.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle.
  • Key settings (starting points)Layer 0.08–0.12 mm, 100 % infill (it is a thin panel), slow outer wall, no supports, no ironing, a typical thickness range of roughly 0.6 mm (bright) to 3–4 mm (dark).
  • OrientationUpright, with the image facing the front for best surface quality; alternatively flat on the plate for a smoother face (the back gets the texture instead).
  • Time classMedium; mostly slow thin layers.
  • PitfallsStrong contrast in the source photo clips highlights; a part-cooling fan too high causes layer lines; tall thin panels wobble.
  • FinishingTest with a bright LED or phone flashlight; add a base or frame so it stands.

Recipe 8 · Large flat tray

  • GoalA tray that fills much of the plate, flat and without corner lift. The common dual-nozzle area is 235.5 × 256 mm; a left-only job can use 256 × 256 mm.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: PETG (or ABS/ASA if you need heat resistance).
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle only, so you can use the full 256 mm area.
  • Key settings (starting points)0.6 mm nozzle for speed (typical), layer 0.28–0.30 mm, brim or mouse ears on corners, 3 walls, sparse infill, bed 70–80 °C for PETG, textured PEI, no part cooling on the first layers. For ABS/ASA, use Heating mode and soak.
  • OrientationFlat. Round the footprint corners and add a low rim to resist curling.
  • Time classMedium to long.
  • PitfallsCorner lift from cooling, elephant's foot, using the aux-limited area by accident (the slicer may restrict the plate when the right nozzle is used).
  • FinishingRemove after the plate cools; a flatness check with a ruler.

Recipe 9 · Print-in-place hinge

  • GoalA hinge printed as one assembly that rotates as soon as it is removed.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: PLA or PETG. Optional slot 2: a contrasting color for the pin.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle. The right nozzle's lower speed would change clearances between parts, so avoid mixing them in the same assembly.
  • Key settings (starting points)Layer 0.16–0.20 mm, clearance 0.3–0.4 mm for a 0.4 mm nozzle (typical), slow outer walls, supports off if the design is self-supporting, ironing off.
  • OrientationHinge axis horizontal or vertical according to the design; avoid supports in the gap.
  • Time classShort.
  • PitfallsJoints fused by elephant's foot; too much flow; wet filament that strings across the gap.
  • FinishingWork the hinge open and closed ten times; a drop of lubricant if needed. Clearance background: tolerances and fits.

Recipe 10 · High-speed draft

  • GoalA quick, rough fit-check part to validate dimensions before a final print.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: PLA.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle (full-speed). A high-flow hotend (HF 0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8) is the right tool; Bambu states HF can cut print time by up to 30 %.
  • Key settings (starting points)Speed mode Sport (124 %) or Ludicrous (166 %) if the part and filament tolerate it; layer 0.28–0.32 mm with a 0.6 mm nozzle (typical); 2 walls, 10 % infill, no supports where possible.
  • OrientationWhatever minimizes support and print height; surface finish is not the goal.
  • Time classShort.
  • PitfallsRinging and weak layer bonds at high speed; volumetric flow exceeding the filament's limit; the printer is loud in Sport/Ludicrous.
  • FinishingNone; discard after the fit check.

Recipe 11 · Detail miniature

  • GoalA 28–32 mm-scale tabletop miniature with crisp faces and fine edges.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: PLA (a matte, light color shows detail best). Slot 2: optional second color for a base.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle with the 0.2 mm hotend if you have swapped to one (PLA is fine there); otherwise 0.4 mm with thin layers. Both hotends must be the same diameter. Remember that the 0.2 mm hotend is not compatible with the CF/GF families in the chart.
  • Key settings (starting points)Layer 0.04–0.08 mm on 0.2 mm; 0.08 mm on 0.4 mm; slow outer wall; tree supports with a small contact; cooling high for PLA; arc fitting and "ironing" off.
  • OrientationTilt 15–45° so overhangs are supported by the body rather than by the support structure; keep faces away from the support side.
  • Time classLong for the size.
  • PitfallsSupport scars on the face, thin weapons snapping, stringing between legs.
  • FinishingCareful support removal, primer, paint.

Recipe 12 · Gradient vase

  • GoalA single-wall or two-wall vase with a color gradient from base to rim.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A: slots 1–3 (or 4) in graduated shades of one hue, e.g. dark → mid → light.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle. Many changes mean many flushes; with a second AMS on the right you could shift a subset of colors, but one nozzle keeps the result consistent.
  • Key settings (starting points)Spiral vase mode is single-color, so color gradients usually use a few layer-height-based color changes (the "change filament at layer" function) with the prime tower on; layer 0.2 mm; flush volumes small because the colors are similar.
  • OrientationUpright.
  • Time classMedium; each color step adds a flush.
  • PitfallsVisible color lines are a feature, not a bug; tall tower instability; shallow gradients where hues are too close to see the step.
  • FinishingWaterproof liner if it will hold flowers and water (the print is not guaranteed watertight).

Recipe 13 · Multi-material keycap

  • GoalA keycap with a contrasting legend inlaid into the top face.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: body color, slot 2: legend color. Both PLA for simplicity.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle. The legend and the body must share a nozzle so registration comes from one hotend.
  • Key settings (starting points)Layer 0.08–0.12 mm, slow outer wall, 2–3 top layers, legend depth about 0.4–0.6 mm, small prime tower or purge into infill, several keycaps per plate to share the tower.
  • OrientationLegend face up, the stem pointing down. Tree supports may be needed inside, depending on the stem design.
  • Time classShort per keycap, medium per plate.
  • PitfallsLegend bleeding into the body, tiny features under-extruded at color changes, stem tolerance (typical: fit-check one first).
  • FinishingLight sanding of the stem; do not sand the legend.

Recipe 14 · Enclosure with heat-set inserts

  • GoalA two-part electronics enclosure with M3 threaded inserts for the lid screws.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A slot 1: PETG (indoor) or ASA (heat or UV exposure). Optionally a second slot as a backup.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle.
  • Key settings (starting points)0.4 mm nozzle, layer 0.20 mm, 3–4 walls, 25 % infill, insert holes to the insert maker's size (typically 0.4–0.65 mm under the insert's outer diameter, for example 4.0 mm for a 4.6 mm M3 insert; check the datasheet), add a vertical "pillar" with extra walls around holes.
  • OrientationLid and base each on their largest flat face; avoid bridging the insert holes.
  • Time classMedium.
  • PitfallsInserts pushed in too fast melt the pillar; walls that are too thin split; ABS/ASA lid warps if it is a large flat part (see engineering and chamber).
  • FinishingPress inserts with a soldering iron tip heated to the material's range (PETG lower than ASA), push straight and slowly, stop when flush, let cool before tightening. See also joints and fasteners.

Recipe 15 · Overnight farm-style batch

  • GoalPrint a full plate of identical small parts unattended and have them all succeed.
  • Filament and slotsAMS A: slots 1–2 hold the same brand, type and color so Auto-Refill can hand over when one runs out; the other slots can hold a second color for a later job.
  • Nozzle assignmentLeft nozzle. With a second AMS on the right nozzle you could split the batch across both for a different material, but the speed cap applies.
  • Key settings (starting points)Standard speed (not Ludicrous), a tested filament profile, brim on tall thin parts, first-layer inspection on, enable the AI detections (spaghetti, etc.) and the pause-on-failure options, "Purify Air at Print End" if the exhaust fan is fitted.
  • OrientationArrange evenly; leave gaps for the toolhead and for part removal.
  • Time classLong, intended overnight.
  • PitfallsAn unfinished spool, a wet spool, the first-layer problem you did not see, nozzle and bed not recently cleaned.
  • FinishingRemove after the plate cools; batch inspect; log the result for the next batch.

Adapting a recipe

Change the material

Check the nozzle-compatibility chart first, then the air mode and plate. Re-dry the spool. Treat the new job as a fresh starting point, not an edit.

Change the nozzle

A larger nozzle is faster but coarser; both hotends must have the same diameter. Re-run calibrations after a hotend swap, as prompted.

Change the plate

Smooth/engineering PEI with glue suits TPU and nylons; textured PEI without glue suits TPU too (never glue on textured PEI). Cool Plate is not for TPU.

Pre-flight checklist
  • Filament dry and matched to the profile (in the slicer, the slot's color/type is synced).
  • Plate clean and the correct type chosen in Studio.
  • Nozzle assignment visible in the preview: nothing TPU on the right nozzle.
  • Flush volumes and the prime tower are reviewed.
  • Door and lid closed for ABS/ASA/PC/PA; filter fitted.
Documented anchors you can rely on

Left-only volume 256 × 256 × 260 mm; right-only and common dual area 235.5 × 256 × 256 mm; right nozzle ≤ 200 mm/s; chamber up to 65 °C; speed modes 50 / 100 / 124 / 166 %. Everything else in the recipes above is a starting point to refine.

Key takeaways

  • Think in nozzle assignment first: TPU and fast, precise work on the left; slower support and utility work on the right.
  • A single AMS 2 Pro feeds the left nozzle; plan a second AMS or a track switch before designing right-nozzle workflows.
  • Matching spools in adjacent slots give Auto-Refill for long jobs.
  • Every value here is a starting point; record what works in a filament profile (see settings and profiles).
