Reading the drawings

The figures are schematic. They show the relationship of parts, not exact proportions or exact positions of screws. Where a part’s position is not stated in Bambu’s documentation, the drawing is labeled typical and the text says so. The Bambu Lab wiki has photographs and step-by-step service guides.

🔰 Walking around the outside

The X2D is a closed box of 392 × 406 × 478 mm that weighs 16.25 kg. A front door and a top cover give access to the chamber; the top cover is a glass lid, and Bambu notes that the top glass cover is shared with the P2S. A 5-inch touchscreen is mounted on the front. The AMS 2 Pro normally stands on top of the printer, and a spool holder for an external spool sits on the left. At the rear are the exhaust fan, the main power inlet and the 6-pin connectors for accessories. Side intakes feed air into the cabinet; the purge chute lets waste leave the machine.

Side view: the cabinet from the right

From the side you can see how the bed moves in Z and where the rear panel carries the auxiliary extruder and the exhaust fan. The AMS 2 Pro sits on top and its PTFE tubes run down the back to the buffer.

Top view: placement and clearances

Bambu’s placement guide gives a free space of 500 × 700 × 600 mm for the X2D (it does not say which figure is width, depth or height in the extract used here, so check the wiki), with an ambient temperature between 10 °C and 30 °C and humidity below 85%. The reason is airflow: the printer breathes through the side intakes and the rear exhaust fan, and it needs room to open the door and the lid.

🔧 The toolhead and the two hotends

The toolhead is the moving heart of the printer. It carries the left (main) hotend, the right (auxiliary) hotend, the main servo extruder, two filament cutters, the part-cooling fan assembly, two hotend-cooling fans and the lever that triggers the nozzle swap. The toolhead camera also looks from here.

The two hotends are identical and interchangeable: the same structure, a maximum temperature of 300 °C, a quick-release lever that locks the hotend onto a ceramic base carrying the heater and thermistor (there are no terminals to unplug), and a silicone sock with three yellow markers that the AI system uses to see the nozzle. A 0.4 mm hardened-steel nozzle is pre-installed, and a spare hotend is in the toolbox.

Standard nozzles come in 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 mm. High-flow nozzles (0.4, 0.6, 0.8 mm) are sold separately and need firmware 01.01.00.00 or later and Bambu Studio 2.5.3.60 or later. Bambu states up to 30% less time for suitable prints. Dual-nozzle printing needs the same diameter on both sides; mixing sizes is not yet supported.

Swapping left and right is a diagnostic tool

Because both hotends are identical, a clog can be located by swapping them: if the clog follows the hotend, it is in the hotend; if it stays on the same side, suspect the extruder or tube. After reinstalling a hotend, run nozzle offset calibration again so the printer knows where each tip sits.

🔄 The gear-and-trigger nozzle switch

On a conventional tool changer a motor moves the tool. On the X2D there is no extra motor in the switch. The toolhead is already moving in X and Y, so Bambu uses that motion. A lever at the bottom of the toolhead is pushed against a push block mounted on the printer’s lining. A connecting rod and gear train then swing the flow blocker and raise or lower the hotends. The same action can be started from the touchscreen on the Nozzle & Extruder page by choosing Left or Right (Aux). Bambu cites tests of more than a million switch cycles.

Why a blocker at all?

A hot nozzle full of molten plastic drips slowly even when nothing is being pushed. If the idle nozzle hung just 1 mm lower than the active one, it could scrape the print. The blocker and the height difference together keep the idle nozzle out of the work and keep any ooze off the model.

⚙️ The two extruders and the filament routes

Main extruder: a servo (PMSM) in the toolhead

The left hotend is fed by a direct-drive extruder inside the toolhead. Its motor is a PMSM (permanent-magnet synchronous motor) run as a servo: the controller reads the motor’s position continuously and corrects it, instead of just counting steps. Because the load on the motor shows up as electrical resistance, the printer can detect filament tangles, and together with the buffer it also understands when the filament is being held back. The gears are hardened steel, which matters for abrasive fiber-filled plastics. The brief mentions a fast servo sampling loop (about 20 kHz); treat that figure as vendor marketing and rely on the observable behavior instead.

Auxiliary extruder: a stepper on the rear panel

The right hotend is fed by the auxiliary extruder, a stepper-driven extruder mounted at the top of the rear panel. Filament leaves it through a 525 mm white PTFE tube to the auxiliary hotend inlet. A second, 555 mm gray PTFE tube, runs from the chamber inlet to the main hotend inlet. Each tube is held by a locking nut with a washer; when the nut is tight it “clicks”, and it can be tightened with the 8 mm open-end wrench from the toolbox. The auxiliary path has two sensors: one in the auxiliary extruder and one in the right side of the toolhead. The touchscreen shows three states: filament in the aux extruder, in the toolhead, or both.

Never reverse the adapter

The 4-in-1 PTFE adapter (FAZ013-N according to the brief) belongs between the AMS and the buffer and must not be installed the other way round. It adds friction, so avoid it with only two AMS units. A standard white adapter is used; the H2C adapter II is a different part.

🧷 The filament buffer

Between the AMS and the printer sits the buffer, a small module with a spring-and-magnet slider and a Hall sensor. When the extruder pulls filament faster than the AMS can supply it, the slider moves, and the AMS speeds up. When the filament is slack, the slider returns and the AMS slows down. A jam or a spool tangle makes the slider stay at an extreme position, so the buffer also detects tangles and runouts. The buffer has two filament inlets (upper for the left extruder, lower for the right extruder) and two 6-pin ports. According to Bambu’s FAQ, one port is used for the AMS data cable and the other for the exhaust fan cable; the AMS units themselves are daisy-chained through their own two rear 6-pin ports. If you do not use the exhaust fan, either port may be used.

Why the AMS 2 Pro needs a buffer

The AMS 2 Pro feeds in two stages: a feeder motor in each slot and a brushless assist motor in the hub. With two motors in series, a buffer is the shock absorber that keeps them in step with the extruder. This is why the buffer is part of every X2D set-up, not an optional extra.

🌀 Air: fans, vents and filter

The cabinet contains five types of air movers: the part-cooling fan in the toolhead cover, the two hotend-cooling fans, the chamber heating fan on the left, the filtration fan on the right and the exhaust fan on the rear. A chamber thermistor, the door sensor and the material profile decide which of them run. In strong-cooling mode, used for PLA and TPU, the left and right auxiliary part-cooling fans blow on the part, the right fan draws external air, and the rear fan exhausts. In heating mode, used for ABS, ASA, PC and PA, the left heating fan and the right filtration fan recirculate the same air through the filter and heater.

🛏️ Heatbed, plate and calibration hardware

The heatbed heats up to 120 °C and carries the textured PEI plate on magnets, with a positioning block so the plate always sits in the same place. Around the bed are several items you will not see on a basic printer: a marker on the magnet surface for camera calibration, a coil behind the bed that the nozzle offset calibration reads with an eddy-current sensor, a wiper steel sheet on which the nozzles are scraped, and a calibration sticker for the toolhead camera. Foreign-object detection under the bed is available from firmware 01.01.00.00. The plate accepts Bambu’s current 256 × 256 mm plates; first-generation plates are not recognized, so choose “Ignore” or disable plate detection if you insist on using one.

Plate care

If you touch the plate, finger oils reduce adhesion. Wash it with warm water and dish soap, dry it and avoid touching the printing area. For TPU, textured PEI needs no glue stick; other plates may need it.

🏁 Motion system

Two stepper motors at the rear drive a CoreXY belt system. Both motors turn together to move the toolhead diagonally and in opposite directions to move it straight. The X-axis is a hollow steel rod. For Z, three lead screws sit at front left, front right and rear middle and are turned together by one Z motor through a belt, so the bed rises level. A semi-automatic belt tensioner uses a spring at the upper rear to keep belt tension within range. The printer lets you tune the motion in the Calibration menu: motor noise cancellation, vibration compensation and automatic bed leveling are the three you will run most often, especially after lubrication or a transport.

Lubrication and the motion system

Bambu’s periodic maintenance schedule depends on how much you print: for heavy use (more than five hours a day) lubricate the XY rods monthly and the Z axis every three months; for regular use (one to five hours a day), every two and four months; for low use, every three and five months. Use one or two drops of oil per 5 cm on linear shafts and grease on the lead screws and extruder gears. After lubricating, run motor noise cancellation, vibration compensation and automatic bed leveling. The full routine is on Maintenance & troubleshooting.

✂️ Cutters, wiper, purge chute

With two filaments always loaded, the printer must be able to cut and retract cleanly. The toolhead holds two filament cutters, one for each hotend. When the toolhead moves to the middle of the chamber, a cutter stopper extends from the rear so the cutter lever can press against it. Bambu advises checking the blades every 8–12 print rolls (6–10 with carbon- or glass-fiber filaments) and replacing a dull blade. The purge wiper uses a push plate and a receiving plate to sweep purged plastic into the purge chute. The nozzle wiper rubs the tip on the plate steel sheet for a rough wipe on a block and a fine wipe that scrapes 1–2 mm. Later units dropped the silicone wiper pad for the auxiliary nozzle because of an algorithm change; either version is fine.

🔌 Ports, screen and holders

📱

Touchscreen

5 inch, 1280 × 720. Left menu: Homepage, Controls, Filaments, Settings, HMS. Controls include air conditioning, speed, motion, nozzle and extruder, temperatures and light.

💾

USB-A 2.0

For FAT32 or exFAT drives (32–64 GB recommended; one drive only). Needed for video recording and log export; no drive is supplied. Internal timelapse storage has been possible since firmware 01.01.00.00.

🔌

Power inlet

Check the voltage version. High voltage: 200–240 V, 1600 W at 220 V; low voltage: 100–120 V, 1100 W at 110 V. Standby draw is about 7–8 W.

🧵

6-pin ports

On the buffer and on every AMS: data and power for AMS units and the exhaust fan. Use the newer 6-pin cable; the older cable is unstable for AMS 2 Pro.

📶

Wi-Fi

Dual-band (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz). There is no wired Ethernet. Choose cloud mode or LAN Only mode.

🧰

Spool holders

An external spool holder base is preinstalled on the left. Use it for filaments that must not go through the AMS, such as TPU.

🎯 Vision Encoder (optional)

The Vision Encoder, sold separately, is the accessory that enables the motion accuracy calibration. It works together with the toolhead camera to measure how well the toolhead really follows commanded positions. Bambu groups it with the calibration tools in the same menu as the nozzle offset and the high-precision nozzle offset. For everyday printing it is not necessary; it is for people who chase dimensional accuracy.

🛠️ Nozzle and hotend swap: the overview

This is an overview, not a replacement for the step-by-step guide on the Bambu Lab wiki. The principle is the same for any size or material change:

Heat and gloves

Nozzles reach 300 °C. If you must remove plastic from a hot nozzle, use the printer’s Heating Base Maintenance Mode and protect your hands. Do not touch the hot tip, and do not put a cold tool into molten plastic and then pull it by hand.

For smooth operation, a typical owner keeps a spare 0.4 mm hotend, a second plate, a spare cutter set, spare PTFE tubing from Bambu, isopropyl alcohol for the chamber and a soft brush. These are suggestions based on the maintenance guides, not an official list.

✅ Key takeaways

  • The X2D carries two identical, interchangeable hotends; the left one has a servo extruder in the toolhead, the right one is fed by a stepper extruder on the rear panel through a 525 mm PTFE tube.
  • The nozzle switch uses the toolhead’s own motion: a lever hits a push block and gears swing the flow blocker. No additional motor is involved.
  • The buffer, with two inlets and two 6-pin ports, sits between the AMS and the printer; the 4-in-1 adapter goes between AMS and buffer, never the other way round.
  • Air handling has two modes: cooling for PLA and TPU, heating for ABS, ASA, PC and PA, with a three-stage filter.
  • Calibration hardware (coil, sticker, wiper sheet, cameras) is part of the bed area; run the relevant calibrations after maintenance.
  • Keep spare nozzles, cutters, PTFE and a second plate, and follow the lubrication schedule.
