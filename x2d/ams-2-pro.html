AMS 2 Pro in depth

The AMS 2 Pro is a four-slot spool cabinet that stores, feeds, identifies and dries filament for the X2D. This page covers how it is built, how filament travels through it, how to dry safely, how to combine units, what the lights mean and where it stops being the right tool.

🔰 What the AMS 2 Pro is

The AMS (Automatic Material System) is a hardware accessory that holds several spools, picks the one the print needs, feeds it toward the printer and pulls it back when the job asks for another color. The AMS 2 Pro is the second-generation, four-slot unit for the X2D. Compared with a simple spool rack, it adds five things: motorized feeding, RFID identification of Bambu spools, humidity and temperature sensing, two heater-and-fan drying modules and a sealed, desiccant-backed storage compartment.

For a beginner the useful mental model is: a polite spool butler. It does not print and it does not melt anything. It hands the right filament to the printer at the right time, keeps the spools dry, and tells you when something is wrong.

🔧 Anatomy

Open the lid and the top of the unit shows four spool bays. Behind them, a hub merges the four filament paths into one. Underneath, hidden by the mid-frame, are the two drying modules. A sealing rubber ring under the lid keeps the compartment closed. A humidity and temperature sensor sits at the front, between slot 2 and slot 3, and a color-changing silicone desiccant sits in its own pocket.

The slot: feeder, bushings and sensor

Every slot has its own feeder with a motor, drive gears and a filament sensor. The gears run in ceramic-reinforced anti-wear bushings, which keep abrasive filament from eating away the plastic. The feeder is what grabs the filament when you push it in about 2 cm and pulls it back afterwards. The “pre-loading” behavior (filament is grabbed, then retracted slightly so it is ready but not committed) is a convenience of this design.

The hub, the assist motor and the buffer

Behind the slots, the hub merges four paths into one. It has four Hall sensors that detect filament, a magnetic rotary encoder that measures how much filament passes, and a brushless assist motor that pushes filament toward the printer. In practice, loading is a two-stage action: the slot feeder moves filament to the hub, then the assist motor carries it onward. Two motors in series must stay in step with the printer’s extruder, which pulls the filament at a speed of its own. The buffer, mounted next to the printer, is the shock absorber that makes this work: a slider on a spring moves when the extruder pulls hard or lets go, and a Hall sensor reports it so the AMS can speed up or slow down. The same slider detects tangles and runouts.

The conceptual difference

A plain AMS stores filament; an AMS 2 Pro conditions it. If you print only PLA from fresh sealed spools, the extra drying hardware is a convenience. If you print nylon, PETG, PC or any hygroscopic material (one that absorbs water from the air), it changes what you can reliably do.

♨️ Drying inside the AMS 2 Pro

Many plastics absorb moisture. When wet filament is heated, the water becomes steam inside the molten plastic and you see bubbles, popping, stringing, rough surfaces and weak parts. Drying warms the spool to drive the water out. In the AMS 2 Pro, each drying module has a heater, a fan and an NTC temperature sensor; the air circulates inside the compartment, entering near the front and leaving near the hub. Two active vents (bottom intake, rear exhaust) exchange humid air with the room. They are closed when the unit is not drying, and open and close once when drying starts as a self-test; they stay closed while the unit heats up and open once the target temperature is reached.

🌡️

Maximum 65 °C

The ceiling is 65 °C, and it is reached only if the room is at least 25 °C. In a cold room the unit will heat less.

🔌

Needs the 6-pin link

Drying only runs with the 6-pin connection to the printer or a power adapter; it is not a standalone dryer.

🚫

One unit, one temperature

Never dry a high-temperature filament together with low-temperature spools in the same unit, for example ABS with PLA.

Rotating or static drying

Two modes exist, and the choice depends on where the filament sits:

  • Rotating drying is used when the filament is pulled back into the spool or stored. The spool turns 30° every 5 minutes, which exposes all layers of wound filament.
  • Static drying is used when the filament is inserted into the feeder. It is not suitable for filaments whose softening temperature is at or below the drying temperature, such as PLA, PVA, Support PLA/PETG and TPU, because filament held still against the warm feeder can deform.

Typical drying targets (guidance, not a specification)

The numbers below are widely used starting points for common filament families, shown against the AMS 2 Pro’s 65 °C ceiling. Many materials with high drying temperatures (nylon, PC and some PETG-CF blends) sit above or near that ceiling, so the AMS 2 Pro dries them gently and slowly or not completely. A heated oven or a dedicated dryer may be better in those cases. For TPU, Bambu itself gives 70 °C for 8 hours in a forced-air oven, or 75–85 °C for 8 hours on a heatbed, which is above the AMS 2 Pro’s ceiling.

Never dry above the softening point

Bambu’s rule is that the drying temperature must not exceed the filament’s softening temperature, and that the pre-feed cooling check will wait if the chamber is hotter than the filament’s softening temperature. A softened spool can fuse its layers into one block. When unsure, choose a lower temperature and a longer time.

Power, 6-pin cable and drying while printing

One AMS 2 Pro connected to the printer through the 6-pin cable gets its power from the printer and can dry. On the X2D, however, an AMS that is powered by the printer and is drying will prioritize its drying power, which can limit some printer operations. For “print while drying” (firmware 01.01.00.00 or later), power the extra units from the official Switching Adapter (24 V, 4 A, 96 W; AC input 100–240 V), and make sure the drying temperature does not exceed the filament’s softening temperature. Use the new 6-pin cable (2.5 mm connector pitch); the old cable gives unstable connections. Drying can also be started remotely from the Device page of Bambu Studio. Firmware 01.02.00.00 additionally turns the motors off while drying in the chamber, which helps with noise.

📦 Storing spools in the AMS 2 Pro

The sealed compartment and desiccant are designed for storage between prints. Keep these points in mind:

  • Remove the desiccant from its bag before installing it. A color-changing silicone desiccant turns from yellow to purple as it saturates; replace or regenerate it when it has changed color. Bambu’s maintenance guide suggests roughly 4–6 weeks of continuous use as a planning interval.
  • Spool size: width 50–68 mm, diameter 197–202 mm. Cardboard spools should use plastic spools or the spool adapter.
  • Keep the vents unblocked and the lid closed; the sealing ring under the lid is part of the humidity control.
  • The humidity indicator on the screen is a guide, not a lab instrument. If a spool pops and bubbles at the nozzle, dry it regardless of what the number says.

🏷️ RFID, Bambu spools and third-party spools

Bambu spools carry an RFID tag that stores the filament type, color, diameter and other data. Two RFID coils inside the AMS read the tag when the filament is inserted, when the printer starts and when you tap Re-Read. The AMS also estimates the remaining filament from a second read. If a spool runs out in the middle of a print, auto-refill can switch to another spool of the same brand, material and color on the same extruder side.

Third-party spools have no tag. The AMS will mark the slot as unknown; you then set the filament type and color manually in the touchscreen, Bambu Studio or Bambu Handy. Setting the right type is not cosmetic: it selects the temperatures, cooling and flow profile used for that slot. Third-party hard or brittle fiber-filled filaments are best avoided in the AMS, because the path has tight bends. Official PLA-CF, PETG-CF and PAHT-CF are optimized for it.

📥 Loading and unloading filament

Loading into a slot

  1. Prepare the spool. Check that the end is cut cleanly (a diagonal cut helps), that the spool is not tangled and that it fits the spool size limits.
  2. Seat the spool in the slot with the filament end facing the feeder.
  3. Insert about 2 cm of filament into the slot’s inlet. The feeder grabs it, and the AMS pulls it slightly back (pre-loading).
  4. Check the slot in the touchscreen or Studio: the type and color of RFID spools appear; for other spools, set the type and color manually.
  5. Print. The AMS loads the right slot automatically when the job calls for it.

Unloading

At the end of a successful job the AMS automatically retracts the filament. After a cancelled job it does not, so you have to unload manually from the screen. If the filament is stuck, remove the white PTFE tube and pull the filament out by hand; do not pull with force through the hub. For long storage, retract the filament so the spool is sealed in the compartment without a strand hanging into the PTFE.

Feed Assist mode

When you want filament held at the feeder but the spool not pulled back, the AMS 2 Pro supports a Feed Assist mode. It is also how the TPU Feed Assist Module uses an AMS HT as a feeder, with the TPU bypass port. TPU never goes through the standard AMS path.

🧩 Combining units

You can connect more than one AMS to the X2D. The rule set is short, but the order matters.

  • The unit connected directly is AMS A; the next ones are cascaded as B, C, D. They are linked through their own two rear 6-pin ports.
  • One or two AMS units (or one AMS and an external spool) connect directly to the buffer, with no adapter.
  • Two AMS plus an external spool, or three or more AMS, need the 4-in-1 PTFE adapter (FAZ013-N per the brief) between the AMS units and the buffer. The adapter adds resistance, so do not use it with only two units.
  • The maximum is 4 AMS 2 Pro plus 8 AMS HT: 12 units, 24 slots. With one external spool that gives 25 colors.

Slots are not the same as colors per print

If two spools hold the same color and material, they take two slots but count as one color for the print (auto-refill uses them in sequence). Bambu Studio’s filament grouping modes (Filament-Saving, Convenience and Custom) also decide which spools share a nozzle; Convenience and Custom can increase purge waste if you cannot rearrange spools to match.

🧭 Routing to the left and right nozzles

Without extra hardware, each AMS is permanently bound to one nozzle. In the first-time AMS Setup the printer either detects the mode itself with the Hall sensors (needs filament in the AMS, nothing loaded in the printer and an empty buffer) or you assign Left or Right manually; the choice is greyed out once filament has been sent. With one AMS 2 Pro connected to the buffer’s upper inlet, it feeds the left (main) nozzle, and that is the configuration Bambu recommends as the combination.

Track-switch limits to remember
  • PTFE from AMS to switch: at most 700 mm; from switch to buffer: at most 115 mm.
  • No external spool support; TPU and several other materials are excluded.
  • High-precision nozzle offset calibration cannot be run with a switch installed.
  • Changing within the same inlet is slow (the filament is fully retracted to the AMS); changing across inlets is fast.
  • With a single AMS plus the switch, feeding is alternating, not simultaneous.

🧰 Maintenance

The AMS 2 Pro is mostly maintenance-free, but a few items matter. Treat PTFE tubes as consumables and replace them when worn. Check the pneumatic couplers at each spool change. Keep the ceramic funnel and the active support shaft clean, look inside the hub for magnets and debris, refresh the desiccant and update the firmware.

Each spool change

Inspect the PTFE couplers; make sure the filament end is clean; check that nothing hangs on the spool.

Monthly (typical)

Look at the desiccant color; clean the vents of dust; wipe the compartment; keep the lid seal clean.

When feeding gets rough

Clean the ceramic funnel and the support shaft; look for filament dust in the hub; replace a worn PTFE tube.

Firmware

Drying fixes arrived in firmware 01.01.01.00. Keep both the AMS and the printer updated.

🔁 Keeping filament loaded: the “not unloading” trick

Some users keep filament loaded after a print to save the purge that comes from unloading and reloading. On the X2D this is done by editing the machine G-code in Bambu Studio: delete the “pull back filament to AMS” lines (before the G150.3 command) in the Machine end G-code and the flush G-code in Machine start G-code. Bambu warns that it carries risks: clogging, and RFID and remaining-estimate features are disabled when filament is left loaded. Restore the default G-code when you change to a new material.

🚧 Limitations

  • Flexibles: Do not feed TPU or TPE through the AMS path (except the special “TPU for AMS” profile). Use an external spool or the TPU Feed Assist Module.
  • Absorbent PVA should not sit in the AMS for long because it takes up moisture.
  • Hard, brittle fibers from third parties can break in the tight bends.
  • Drying temperature ceiling of 65 °C leaves nylon and PC under-dried.
  • Slots per nozzle: without a track switch, the AMS-to-nozzle binding is fixed.
  • Weak points: PTFE tubes, couplers and the buffer’s slider are wear items that need your attention.

✅ Key takeaways

  • The AMS 2 Pro has four slots, two drying modules (maximum 65 °C, only if ambient ≥ 25 °C), a humidity sensor, active vents, two RFID coils and a color-changing desiccant.
  • Filament moves in two stages (slot feeder, then hub assist motor), and the buffer keeps them in step with the extruder.
  • Never dry a high-temperature spool together with low-temperature spools; never exceed the filament’s softening temperature.
  • One or two AMS connect directly to the buffer; three or more (or two plus an external spool) need the 4-in-1 PTFE adapter, installed between AMS and buffer.
  • The listed maximum is 4 AMS 2 Pro and 8 AMS HT plus one external spool, for 25 colors.
  • Without the optional track switch each AMS is tied to one nozzle.
  • TPU, absorbent PVA and brittle third-party fiber filaments are poor fits for the AMS path.
